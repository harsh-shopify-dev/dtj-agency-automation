## 1.2 Behavioural & physical micro-analysis (timestamped)

**0.0–2.4s — the wave**
- Right arm is already **fully extended above head** at frame 1. No wind-up. The ad starts mid-gesture.
- He is **flapping the paperback back and forth** by its cover — the book is bent open and floppy, which visually sells "thin/tiny" before a single word about page count.
- Eyebrows **raised and held** (frontalis engaged, forehead creased). Eyes wide, sclera visible above the iris.
- Mouth in a **closed-lip half-smile pulling to his left** — the "I know something you don't" smirk.
- Head is tilted ~8° toward the raised arm. Chin slightly down, so he's looking *up into* the lens.

**2.4–3.4s — the drop**
- Book exits frame bottom-left. Hand does **not** come back into shot for ~1s. Frame goes clean the instant the `88 PAGES` caption hits, so nothing competes with the number.
- Eyelids relax to neutral; brow stays half-raised. He holds a **micro-nod** on "88."

**3.4–4.9s — the savour face**
- On "dwell on every page," both **eyes fully close** for ~0.5s, **glabellar furrow** (vertical crease between brows), **nose scrunch**, lips pushed forward and slightly parted.
- This is the single most important expression in the ad: it performs *"this line hit me"* without saying it. It is the emotional proof.
- Left hand rises into frame bottom holding the book; index finger extended, thumb up — a soft point, not a jab.

**4.9–5.7s — the pause**
- Full 0.84s of dead air. Eyes reopen, look **slightly off-lens to camera-left**, then snap back. Reads as recall, not script.

**5.7–7.5s — the lean-in / look-down**
- Camera drifts **closer and lower**; his head fills more of the frame and slides to frame-left. He **tilts his head down and drops his eyes** to the book off-screen, as if actually finding page 34.
- Mouth stays slightly open between words. No smile here — the face goes neutral-serious for the quote.

**7.5–9.9s — page macro #1**
- Hard cut. The **open page is held between camera and face**, top-of-frame. Only his **eyes, nose and mouth** are visible under the page edge. This is the format's signature shot: the reader is literally behind the text.
- Thumb pins the page at the right margin. Slight handheld wobble kept in.

**9.9–14.6s — page macro #2 + page turn**
- Second cut to a **different setup entirely**: book held up against a window, daylight from behind, **green foliage and a white grille visible** through the glass. Warm, backlit, editorial.
- ~11.0s: thumb **flips a spread**, revealing the next chapter head. The turn is the only motion — camera is nearly locked.
- Frames 12–15 hold on the spread for ~3.5s with almost no movement. **Long hold = confidence.**

**14.6–15.2s — return to talking head**

**15.2–17.6s — THE PRODUCT REVEAL**
- Arm raises again, but this time the book is **cover-out, flat, held steady at forehead height** and pinned there for a full 2.4 seconds. It does not wave. Waving = "look how thin." Static hold = "read the title."
- **Eyebrows shoot up. Eyes widest of the whole video. Mouth opens into a round "oh."** Full-face enthusiasm — the only moment he performs excitement rather than reverence.
- ~17.0s a small hand shake causes visible motion blur on the cover. Left in. Reads as unrehearsed.

**17.6–19.4s — the settle**
- Book drops. He **leans back**; framing goes noticeably **wider** (head + full shoulders, door and wall art enter frame). Body language downshifts from "pitch" to "explain" for the credential line.

**19.4–21.9s — full-screen context B-roll**
- Cut away from the creator entirely. First a **3-panel vertical stack**: an engraving of Epictetus at a desk / a title card reading `EPICTETUS` beside a marble bust / a painted scene of him teaching a crowd.
- Then a single full-screen **desaturated double-exposure** of a marble bust fused with Greek columns.
- Purpose: borrow institutional authority for 2.5 seconds without the creator having to claim it.

**21.9–26.4s — the close**
- Back to talking head, **medium-wide**, relaxed. Eyebrows do a small **double-raise** on "read it whenever you want."
- ~25.5–26.2s: raises the book one last time showing the **back cover / barcode**, then drops it immediately. A throwaway — reinforces "real object I own," not a stock render.

**26.4–27.67s — black end card.**

## 1.3 Pacing, tone & energy map

| Beat | Window | Function | Energy (1–10) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hook | 0.0–2.4s | Impossible claim + prop wave | **8** |
| Stat | 2.4–4.9s | Justify the claim with a number | 7 |
| Savour | 3.4–4.9s | Emotional proof (the closed-eye face) | 5 |
| Breath | 4.9–5.7s | Reset. Makes the next line feel found, not read | 2 |
| Proof | 5.7–14.6s | **Read one real line off one real page** | 4 (deliberately flat) |
| Reveal | 15.2–17.6s | Title + author, static hold | **9** (peak) |
| Credential | 17.6–19.9s | "…considered the beginning of Stoicism" | 6 |
| Authority B-roll | 19.4–21.9s | Borrowed institutional weight | — |
| Benefit | 20.2–24.4s | Pocket size, read anytime | 5 |
| CTA | 24.4–26.4s | Soft follow, no hard sell, **no link, no price** | 4 |
| End card | 27.0–27.7s | Brand-free black card | — |

**Two structural facts that make this format work, and that most people copy wrong:**
1. **The proof section is the longest beat (8.9s of 27.7s = 32%) and the lowest energy.** The ad spends a third of its runtime just reading you a sentence off a page. That drop in energy after a high hook is what makes it feel like a recommendation instead of an ad.
2. **There is no CTA to buy.** No price, no link, no "grab your copy." The only ask is "follow." Trust-first. For paid conversion you will need to add a CTA — put it in the *caption/primary text*, not the video, or the video stops feeling organic.

## 1.5 Voice & speech pattern (the thing to actually replicate)

- **Rate:** ~155 wpm. Mid-tempo. Not the fast hype-cadence of typical BookTok.
- **Register:** conversational Indian English. Full sentences. Zero filler ("um," "like," "basically" all absent).
- **Dynamics:** narrow. He never shouts and never whispers. The energy shifts come from **face and framing, not volume.**
- **Emphasis:** lands on numbers and proper nouns — "**88** pages," "page number **34**," "**Epictetus**," "**Stoicism**." Slight pitch rise on each, then settle.
- **Clause endings:** small upward lilt, which keeps you waiting for the next clause. It's why there's only one pause in 27 seconds.
- **Quote delivery:** when reading the page, the voice **flattens and slows** — no performance, no drama. He reads it like it's obvious. That restraint is what sells it.
- **The single pause (4.86–5.70s)** is the whole trick. One breath, placed right before "For example." It converts a script into a thought.

> **Note on voice casting:** the reference creator is male. Every one of the 22 existing UGC assets in `ads/UGC/` uses this same talking-head-with-book setup, roughly 2:1 female to male. The delivery spec above is gender-neutral — the only thing that must carry over is **narrow dynamics + one real pause + flat quote reading**. A female VO should sit around 150–160 wpm with the same restraint; resist the urge to add warmth or lilt to the quoted lines, because the flatness is the proof.

# PHASE 3 — SHARED PRODUCTION BIBLE

Every concept below uses the **same 6-beat skeleton** lifted from the reference. Only the angle and one production variable change per concept.

```
0.0 – 2.5s   HOOK      Contrarian claim + physical prop move. Caption in black box.
2.5 – 5.0s   STAT      The number/fact that justifies it. Kinetic display caption.
5.0 – 5.8s   BREATH    One real pause. Eyes off-lens and back.
5.8 – 15.0s  PROOF     Read ONE real line off ONE real page. Cut to page macro.
                       Energy DROPS here. Flat delivery. No music.
15.0 – 19.5s REVEAL    Book cover-out, held STATIC at forehead height, 2.5s.
                       Title + author + one credential line.
19.5 – 22.0s CONTEXT   Full-screen B-roll (see per-concept). Borrowed authority.
22.0 – 26.5s BENEFIT   Why you specifically should read it. Soft.
26.5 – 28.0s END CARD  Black. "read it before you recommend it" / "follow for more"
```

**Locked specs across all 12:**
- **Length:** 26–30s. Do not exceed 30.
- **Camera:** handheld selfie, arm's length, front camera, un-flipped. Constant micro-drift. 1080×1920 shot, **export at ~2.5 Mbps** so it doesn't look produced.
- **Lighting:** warm soft ambient from above-left. **No ring light.** If the room has a ceiling cove or a warm lamp, use it and let it be uneven.
- **B-roll:** shoot every page macro **at a window in hard cool daylight** with visible greenery/sky behind. This cool/warm split is non-negotiable — it's what makes the inserts feel like evidence.
- **Page macro rule:** hold the open page between camera and face so only eyes/nose/mouth show beneath it. Then a second setup at the window. Include **one real page turn.**
- **Audio:** voice only under the talking head. No music. Music is allowed **only** under the context B-roll (19.5–22.0s), then out.
- **Captions:** only the first ~7 seconds. Hook = white on solid black rounded box, 2 lines, upper third. Stat = heavy outlined display caps, top of frame, no box. Nothing after that until the end card.
- **No price, no link, no "buy now" in the video.** All commerce lives in the ad's primary text.
- **Page numbers:** the book's printed page number = PDF page − 12. Both are given below. **Verify on the physical copy before the shoot** and shoot the page macro on the real page — the number must match what's visible.

# PHASE 5 — SHOOT ORDER & TESTING

**Batch 1 (test the format):** Concepts 1, 3, 9 — one quiet, one funny, one loud. Same creator, same day, three wardrobe changes. If the format works at all, it works here.
**Batch 2 (test the segments):** Concepts 2, 5, 10 — health, family, control. These are the three biggest matrix clusters.
**Batch 3 (widen):** Concepts 4, 7, 11 — self-worth, friendship, travel. Different audiences entirely, cheap to test.
**Batch 4 (the heavy ones):** Concepts 6, 8, 12 — lowest energy, highest emotional ceiling, most likely to be the winners on watch-time.

**What to actually read in the metrics:** this format's whole bet is the 5.8–15s proof section. If **hold rate at 15s** is under ~35%, the quote you picked isn't good enough — swap the page, keep everything else. If 3s hook rate is fine but 15s collapses, that's a page problem, not a hook problem.

**One page number per ad. Say it twice. Put it on the end card.** The page number is the campaign's only mechanic — it turns a scroll into a task.
