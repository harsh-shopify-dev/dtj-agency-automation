# Copywriting: make the shopper feel the product before they buy it

Harsh, 10 Oct 2026, on MojoVibe's hot sauce page (mojovibe.in, Honey Ghost Chilli + Spicy Habanero combo): its short description
"gives the experience just reading the line ... whatever the category, the copy should kill it." This file is the method behind
lines like that, for ANY category, and the truth rules that keep them safe. Read it in P2 (collect the sensory material), P3
(write the voice lines in the plan) and P4 (each section's words). Quote competitors only to study them, never on our pages.

## 1. Why the MojoVibe line works (taken apart)

The buy-box line, in short: an enemy (it is made to destroy boring meals), then "kissed by honey, slapped by fire", then a payoff
(your recipe becomes unforgettable). Six moves in about 30 words:

| Move | In MojoVibe's line | Why it sells |
|---|---|---|
| 1. A sensory paradox | sweet AND fierce heat, in one breath | The product's real USP (honey + ghost chilli) becomes a feeling, not a spec |
| 2. Personified, physical verbs | honey *kisses*, fire *slaps* | The reader's body reacts: you almost taste it. Verbs do the work, not adjectives |
| 3. Parallel structure | "kissed by X, slapped by Y" | Two equal halves = rhythm = memorable; it reads like a tagline |
| 4. A named enemy | boring meals, chaotic marination | The villain is a situation the shopper hates, never a competitor |
| 5. A payoff in the shopper's life | the recipe becomes unforgettable | The last words are about the shopper's win, not the product |
| 6. Every image stands on a true fact | honey is in it; ghost chilli is very hot | The metaphor is a dramatised fact, so it never needs a disclaimer |

The rest of their page repeats the same toolkit (paraphrased, not their words):
- **Rhythm triplets:** three short imperative beats that escalate, often with alliteration ("drip, dip, dominate").
- **Before and after in one phrase:** from bland to bold, with the effort in it (one squeeze).
- **Identity lines:** who it is NOT for, then who it IS for. It flatters the buyer and filters the wrong one.
- **Mock warnings:** a joke written like a label warning. It shows confidence and makes people smile.
- **Feature, dash, attitude:** every plain feature gets a punchy consequence after a dash ("real peppers, no fake heat").
- **Badge plus twist:** a plain label with a witty second line ("Balanced heat / flavour first, then fire").
- **Local, concrete use scenes:** Maggi, momos, paneer tikka, dal rice. Specific Indian food, not "your favourite dishes".
- **Taste over time:** how the heat builds after a few bites. Experience as a timeline, not a single adjective.
- **Voice in boring places:** storage, allergen and pro-tip text stay in character ("the olive oil doing its thing").
- **Reviews with a dish and a sensation:** each quote names what they cooked and what it felt like.

## 2. What NOT to copy from the same page (it is why we check facts)

Their voice is excellent; some of their claims are not safe. We keep the voice and drop the claims:
- "No Preservatives" and "100% Natural" badges, while the ingredient list on the same page includes Sodium Benzoate (INS 211), a
  preservative. **Every badge is checked against the ingredient list and label** (facts-intake checklist).
- "Vegan Friendly" on a honey sauce, while their own FAQ says the honey sauce is not vegan. **One product, one truth.**
- "Lose Weight By Eating Well" printed on a product picture: a weight-loss claim for a sauce. **Never** (ASCI; our rule 5).
- "India's only", "Chef Approved", "Restaurant Quality": superlatives and endorsements need proof we hold.
- The ticker repeats the same five punchlines four times. A punchline works once or twice, then it is wallpaper.

## 3. The method (any product, any category)

Write these seven lines BEFORE the section copy. They go in `wireframe-plan.md` under "Voice lines", each with the fact it stands on.

1. **The true core.** One physical experience the product creates: what it tastes, feels, smells, sounds or looks like, or
   the moment it changes the shopper's day. Find it in reviews (the words customers use), the pack, the founder's story.
2. **The paradox pair.** Two qualities that normally fight but this product has both. Personify each with a physical verb, in
   parallel halves: "<verb>-ed by <quality A>, <verb>-ed by <quality B>" or "<does A> like <X>, <does B> like <Y>".
3. **The enemy.** The everyday situation the shopper is tired of (the 3 pm slump, frizz by noon, a bathroom that smells after
   cleaning). Never a competitor's name.
4. **The payoff.** The shopper's win in their own life, in one concrete picture (guests ask for the recipe; you finish the deck
   before 5).
5. **The rhythm line.** Three beats, verbs first, escalating, ideally alliterative.
6. **The identity line.** "Not for <the wrong buyer>. Made for <the right one>." Playful, never insulting a group.
7. **The before and after.** "From <state now> to <state after> in <one small effort>."

Then build the short description (buy box intro) as: **enemy, paradox, payoff**, at most 30 words, two sentences, no spec.

## 4. The voice dial (the vibe decides how loud)

| Vibe | How far to go | Sounds like (original examples, not from any brand) |
|---|---|---|
| electric, playful | All seven moves; mock warnings and identity lines welcome | "Pop it. Melt it. Own the afternoon." |
| fresh, appetite | Sensory paradox and use scenes; light humour | "Crunch first, then the slow citrus." |
| calm, natural | Sensory and slow; no shouting, no warnings | "Soft as a well-washed tee, warm as the first sun." |
| premium, editorial | Few words, one precise image; no jokes | "Weightless on the wrist. Impossible to ignore." |
| clinical | Precise sensation + mechanism; no metaphor that sounds like a promise | "Feels like water. Works like a serum." |

## 7. Truth rules (code checks some; you check the rest)

- **Every voice line stands on a fact.** Write the fact next to it in the plan. In `facts.json` its source starts with
  `DTJ COPY (basis: <the fact's source>)`, so the build report lists it under `dtj_copy` for the client's sign-off (lint L19).
- A metaphor may dramatise a fact (honey is sweet, so "kissed by honey"); it may not add one ("melts fat", "boosts immunity").
- Numbers only from the facts (lint L08). No new superlative, endorsement, award or "only" (lint L19 risky words, unless the
  brand's own facts say it with a source).
- Check every badge against the ingredient list and the label: "no preservatives", "100% natural", "vegan", "sugar-free".
- Jokes must fit the category's risk. "May cause addiction" is a joke on a sauce and a real worry on a caffeine product: never
  use dependency, overdose, side-effect or body-image jokes for supplements, beverages with caffeine, medicines or skincare.
- Never mock the shopper, a body type, a region or a community. Identity lines flatter the buyer, they do not insult anyone.
- Indian rules: ASCI on claims and comparisons; FSSAI wording for food and nutraceuticals.
- **A claim never gets stronger on its way into our copy** (borrowed from the `dtj-product-page-offer-meta-ad-copy` skill's
  claim-validation rules, 10 Oct 2026). Keep the brand's qualifier: "helps visibly brighten" -> "brighter-looking skin", never
  "visibly brighter skin"; "hydrates" never becomes "never dry" / "without the dryness"; "water-resistant" never "waterproof";
  "dermatologically tested" never "safe for all skin"; "no added sugar" never "sugar-free"; "4.6 from 1,284 reviews" never "India's
  favourite". "Plausible" is not "supported": if the brand does not say it, it is not written (Hope N Skin draft 1 said "without the
  dryness"; fixed).
- Category no-go list for our lines (same source): beauty and skincare: cures, treats, heals, permanent, removes, fairness or
  lightening, "safe for all skin", before/after implications; hair: regrowth, stops hair fall; food: weight loss, immunity,
  "healthy" or "guilt-free" as absolutes; supplements: treat, cure, prevent, time-bound results.

## 8. Self-check for every line (write "ok" or the fix)

1. Can the reader feel, taste, see or picture something? (A spec alone fails.)
2. Is there a verb doing the work? (Swap weak adjectives for verbs.)
3. Is it true, and which fact does it stand on?
4. Could a competitor say the exact same line? If yes, it is generic: add the product's own paradox or detail.
5. Is it short enough for its slot (table in section 6)?
6. Read it aloud: does it have a rhythm (two halves, or three beats)?
7. Does it say it once? (No punchline on the page twice.)

## 9. Worked example: Go Mints Energy + Focus (electric, playful)

Facts used: caffeine 50 mg and L-theanine 75 mg per mint (L-theanine is the calm compound found in tea), melts in the mouth, the
brand says it works in about 4 minutes, pocket strip of 12, coffee or peppermint flavour, zero sugar (gomints.in, 10 Oct 2026).

| Move | Line | Stands on |
|---|---|---|
| Paradox (eyebrow) | "Coffee's kick. Tea's calm." | caffeine + L-theanine (tea's calm compound); the brand's "calm focus" |
| Short description | "Made for the afternoon slump: caffeine that wakes you like coffee, L-theanine that steadies you like tea. No cup, no queue." | each image names the ingredient it stands on; no dose, time or amount added |
| Rhythm (strip 2) | "Pop it. Melt it. Own the afternoon." | the use steps |
| Identity (strip 2) | "Not for nap-takers. Made for deadline-chasers." | for work, study, drives (brand) |
| Before and after (final push) | "From zoned out to locked in, in about 4 minutes." | the brand's "works in 4 minutes" |
| Feature, dash, attitude (why-us) | "50 mg natural caffeine: the kick, minus the cup." | dose (pack and site) |
| Mock warning | NOT used: a dependency joke on a caffeine product breaks rule 7 | |

Compare the v4 page's plain description, "Caffeinated, mouth-dissolving mints that deliver energy and focus in minutes": true,
but nothing to feel. The rewrite keeps every fact and adds the experience.

Two lines were rejected while writing this example, and why (do the same check on every line):
- "...melts in your mouth before the kettle boils": a dissolving time the brand never states. Cut.
- "Made for the 3 pm slump": "3 pm" is a number no fact gives (lint L08 would only pass it because a SAMPLE review says it). Use
  "the afternoon slump".
