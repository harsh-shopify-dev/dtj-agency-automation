# Hook fast, prove plainly, label the AI

The best short-form ad scriptwriters of 2024-2026 write the script as a run of earned seconds. A three-layer hook (spoken line, opening visual, on-screen text) has to say something relevant to the viewer within 1-3 seconds. The body gives a new reason to keep watching every 5-15 seconds. Each proof answers one objection, and a clear, specific call to action (CTA) comes once mid-video and again at the end. DTJ's own measurements of 20 Blue Tea India reels with speech show what this looks like in Indian Hindi-first advertising. Speech starts on frame one in every reel, about 9.5 words are spoken in the first 3 seconds, the average pace is about 2.8 words a second, the voiceover is Hindi with English product words, and the median length is 36 seconds. Presenter reels cut slowly but change a graphic every 1-2 seconds. Since December 2025, Meta has said it groups ads that look and feel alike into one creative. The unit DTJ tests is therefore the *concept* (a different persona, format and storyline), not the opening line. DTJ's bigger risk is legal, not creative. Blue Tea's longest-running ads lean on weight-loss promises, "100% natural" lines, before/after bodies and AI re-enactments. FSSAI's May 2025 "100%" advisory, the Drugs and Magic Remedies Act, Meta's health policy and ASCI's September 2026 rules for AI-made content all forbid these or require labels on them, and the ASCI rules take effect around 29 December 2026. The practical rule for DTJ: copy Blue Tea's mechanics (format, pace, captions, a named enemy, one ingredient per job) and never its claims. Script AI characters as presenters, demonstrators or cartoons labelled "Video created using AI", never as customers reporting results.

A note on evidence. External findings are cited with links. Items marked **DTJ's own analysis** come from DTJ's frame-by-frame breakdowns and measured speech numbers of Blue Tea India ads downloaded from Meta Ad Library on 21 October 2026. These are first-hand observations, not published research. "Days running" is how long each ad had been live when it was collected. It is a proxy for performance, because advertisers rarely keep paying for losers, but it is not a measured return on ad spend.

## The first three seconds fire three layers, and relevance beats raw attention

Practitioners agree that a hook is three things fired together: the first spoken line, what is on screen in the first 1-3 seconds, and the first text the eye lands on. Motion's framework rates whether these three layers align. It also sets a simple test: within 1-3 seconds at least one of four things must happen. These are an open loop, something that feels about the viewer, a pattern interrupt, or a clear promise of what the next 30 seconds give ([Motion, Hook Analysis](https://motionapp.com/library/frameworks/hook-analysis)). The same framework warns about balance. Too much clarity removes the reason to keep watching, and too much curiosity reads as clickbait. Savannah Sanchez, whose agency ships 200+ Meta and TikTok ads a week, calls the hook "the most important variable that you can change in your ad". She warns that changing only the text on top is not seen by Facebook as a new variation ([Motion: Savannah Sanchez](https://motionapp.com/library/expert/savannah-sanchez/)).

The measured numbers support that focus but come with two strong caveats. On Meta, "hook rate" (or thumbstop) is 3-second plays divided by impressions. Motion's bands are **below 25% needs work, 25-35% solid, above 35% strong** ([Motion help, metrics cheat sheet](https://help.motionapp.com/en/articles/7730931-metrics-cheat-sheet); [AdSights 2026 benchmarks](https://www.adsights.ai/resources/guides/video-creative-metrics-benchmarks-2026-guide)). Secondary 2026 roundups put cold in-feed DTC medians near 23%, cold Reels near 30% and retargeting near 36%. AdSights itself calls these "directional", because Meta publishes no official benchmark ([AdSights](https://www.adsights.ai/resources/guides/video-creative-metrics-benchmarks-2026-guide)). Motion adds one hard rule: if first-frame retention is under 90%, change the first frame. In practice that means never opening on a logo card, a black frame or a slow fade ([Motion help](https://help.motionapp.com/en/articles/7730931-metrics-cheat-sheet)). TikTok's current help-centre guidance says to state the proposition **within the first 3 seconds** and land the hook **within 6** ([TikTok Ads Help](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/creative-best-practices)). Its older research found that **over 63% of its highest-CTR auction ads show the key message or product in the first 3 seconds** ([TikTok for Business, 2020](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/en-US/blog/9-creative-tips-to-drive-auction-ad-performance)). The caveats come from two respected practitioners. Barry Hott's team has seen winning ads with **hook rates under 15%** and argues "Nobody cares what you have to say unless it relates to them clearly and instantly" ([Alex Cooper on Barry Hott](https://alexcooper.beehiiv.com/p/7-facebook-advertising-lessons-i-learned-from-barry-hott)). Dara Denney says the creator "is more important candidly than the script" ([Motion: Dara Denney](https://motionapp.com/library/expert/dara-denney/)). A clever hook that reaches the wrong person is a failure, and a high hook rate on its own is not success.

Which openers win most often? The largest dataset found is Motion's Creative Benchmarks 2026: **550,000+ Meta ads, 6,000+ advertisers, about $1.3B in spend, September 2025 to early January 2026**. A "winner" there is an ad that spends 10x the account median. By hook type, **newness hooks hit 11.37%, urgency 9.73%, offer-only 9.29%, confession 8.74%, curiosity 7.77%, bold claim 7.19% and relatability 6.85%** ([Motion / Runneth Creative Benchmarks 2026](https://runneth.com/thumbstop-pulse/creative-benchmarks-2026/)). The window includes Black Friday, which favours offer and urgency hooks, and in India both must be literally true (see the claims section).

**DTJ's own analysis** of Blue Tea shows how an Indian category leader applies this. In all 20 reels with speech, **the first word is spoken at 0.0 seconds**. The first 3 seconds carry a median of **9.5 words (8-11)**, about 3.2 words a second, faster than the reel's 2.8 average. So the hook is one full, fast sentence with no silent build-up ([DTJ style.json](file:///D:/CODE%20CONTAINERR/WEB%20DEV/dtj-reel-engine/docs/ugc-reels/libraries/blue-tea/style.json)). ## Every proven structure fills the same five beats

Underneath the named formats sits one skeleton. SideShift's 2026 template guide times it as: **hook 0-3 s; context 3-10 s (who it is for, why the speaker cares); value 10-25 s (demo, story or list); proof 25-35 s (result, timeframe or on-camera demonstration); CTA in the final 5 s**, spoken and shown on screen. It budgets 60-110 words of talking points for a 30-45 second ad. When a script runs long, cut context first and never the hook or the CTA ([SideShift](https://sideshift.app/blog/ugc-script-template)). TikTok's own structure is the same idea in three steps: "Have a hook, highlight your unique selling points, and end with a clear call-to-action" ([TikTok Ads Help](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/creative-best-practices)). Billo calls hook, problem, solution, CTA the core UGC format, at about 75-120 words for 30 seconds ([Billo](https://billo.app/?p=45545)).

The named structures differ in how they fill the middle. SideShift catalogues twelve, each with its funnel stage and length. They include problem-solution (cold, 20-35 s), testimonial with one minor drawback (warm, 30-45 s), demo (30-60 s), unboxing (20-40 s), before/after (15-30 s), three reasons (25-40 s), comparison (bottom of funnel, 30-60 s), routine (30-60 s), myth-busting (20-35 s), storytime (30-60 s), objection handling (price, use case, "what's the catch", 30-50 s) and POV (10-20 s) ([SideShift](https://sideshift.app/blog/ugc-script-template)). Alex Cooper, after **$3M spent on founder ads**, names five founder formats. They are Ugly Founder (phone to camera, unscripted, product late), Founder Story, Podcast Founder (an industry truth first), Warehouse Founder and Customer Call ([Alex Cooper](https://alexcooper.beehiiv.com/p/i-spent-3m-on-founder-ads-here-are-the-5-founder-ads-that-work)). Motion adds a separate layer of "mechanics", the mental move that makes an idea land, which works with any structure. Examples are the Borrowed Enemy (pin the problem on a category), the Reframe (agree with a belief, then shift it), the Trojan Horse (looks like entertainment until the last ~20%), the Social Witness and Contrast Without Comment ([Motion Creative Mechanics](https://motionapp.com/library/creative/mechanics/)). Motion's working stack is **Messaging Angle -> Creative Mechanic -> Hook -> Visual Format**. Teams that keep those four words apart can describe a brief exactly.

**DTJ's own analysis** maps neatly onto this vocabulary. Blue Tea's 25 collected files are **21 unique videos across about 15 format families**. Almost every one runs on a named enemy: green tea, bitter caffeinated tea, celebrity and TV ads, or "brands that sold you bitter tea in the name of health" (#1, #3, #8, #12, #23). That is Motion's Borrowed Enemy used as house style. The "Tera kuch nahi hoga / Or ab??" meme (#7, 121 days) is a Trojan Horse that reads as organic until its last 10 seconds. The warehouse Q&A (#17, #19) is Cooper's Warehouse Founder with an inbox question on top. Blue Tea also has a fixed idea of the hero shot: **the drink in a clear glass cup, not the pack**. The colour-change brew (hot water turning blue, lemon turning it purple) is its signature proof shot, and the pouch mostly appears only at the end ([DTJ Senso playbook](file:///D:/CODE%20CONTAINERR/WEB%20DEV/dtj-reel-engine/docs/ugc-reels/brands/senso/blue-tea-playbook.md); [DTJ breakdown #14-25](file:///D:/CODE%20CONTAINERR/WEB%20DEV/dtj-reel-engine/docs/ugc-reels/libraries/blue-tea/breakdown_14_25.md)).

For the script document itself, experienced practitioners keep the strategy (the brief) separate from the execution (the script). SideShift's six-block brief puts claims, banned words and disclosure in a **boundaries block of its own** ([SideShift](https://sideshift.app/blog/ugc-script-template)). For human creators they write scenes and talking points, not word-for-word lines ([Billo](https://billo.app/?p=45545); [Influee](https://influee.co/blog/ugc-brief-template)). DTJ's AI reels are the exception. An AI voice reads exactly what it is given, so the script has to be exact lines, written in spoken register. A workable layout is one row per beat with columns for time, visual, spoken line, on-screen text, insert or B-roll, the proof element, and the claim source for every claim line. The hook gets 3-5 alternate rows while the body rows stay fixed. That layout is a synthesis of the brief sources above, not one agency's published standard. No agency's published column layout was found.

## Write for the ear at about 2.8 words a second

Voiceover vendors put normal ad pace at about **150 words a minute (2.5 words a second)**. TikTok and Reels reads run 150-180, and hard-sell reads 180-200 ([Rows script calculator](https://rows.com/calculators/video-script-calculator); [Bread n Beyond](https://breadnbeyond.com/how-many-words-does-a-60-seconds-explainer-video-needs/)). Blue Tea's measured median is **2.77 words a second (about 166 a minute), with a 20-80% range of 1.7-3.5** (**DTJ's own analysis**, [style.json](file:///D:/CODE%20CONTAINERR/WEB%20DEV/dtj-reel-engine/docs/ugc-reels/libraries/blue-tea/style.json)). That gives DTJ firm word budgets: about 40 words for a 15-second reel, 80-85 for 30 seconds and 95-100 for Blue Tea's 36-second median. Of those, 9-11 words go in the first 3 seconds. The same file measures about **one question to the viewer per 30 seconds**, **4.1 uses of "you" per 100 words** and **zero filler words**.

That last number contradicts a popular piece of advice. Sarah Levinger says "If you want it to be natural, do not script it" ([Motion: Sarah Levinger](https://motionapp.com/library/expert/sarah-levinger/)). Sanchez scripts first-time creators but lets them ad-lib ([Motion talk, Jul 2026](https://motionapp.com/library/talk/15-proven-ugc-ad-hooks-that-are-working-right-now-savannah-sanchez/)). Yet Blue Tea's winners are tightly scripted reads with no "umm" or "like". The two can be reconciled: the natural feel comes from *register*, not from mistakes. Short spoken units, everyday code-mixed Hindi, real situations, one direct question and the speaker's own point of view make a script sound like a person. Fake fillers in an AI voice would more likely sound staged. That is DTJ's inference, not a measured result. Blue Tea's median "sentence" measures 16.6 words, but the 20-80% range of 10-43 words suggests the transcription tool joins Hindi clauses loosely. DTJ should treat it as a measuring artefact and still write each caption chunk at 1-4 words and each spoken unit at roughly 4-12 words. The sources give no measured ideal sentence length for UGC ads.

The words themselves should come from customers, not from brand copy. Hott pushes clear over clever and customers' own language ([Northbeam on Barry Hott](https://northbeam.io/post/unleashing-the-power-of-ugly-ads)). Levinger mines Reddit and reviews by emotion because people are open about problems there ([Foreplay summary](https://www.foreplay.co/post/harnessing-psychology-in-creative-strategy-sarah-levingers-insights-on-consumer-behavior)). TikTok asks for "short, direct, personal language" ([TikTok for Business](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/en-US/blog/9-creative-tips-to-drive-auction-ad-performance)). In Hindi and Gujarati that means Hindi or Gujarati grammar with the English words people actually use: metabolism, cravings, caffeine free, order, skin. Blue Tea's voiceovers are a **99% Hindi median** with exactly those English product words ([DTJ style.json](file:///D:/CODE%20CONTAINERR/WEB%20DEV/dtj-reel-engine/docs/ugc-reels/libraries/blue-tea/style.json)). Wakefit's tagline "Lafda tab hota, jab tum barabar nai sota" shows the colloquial spelling that reads as a person talking ([afaqs](https://www.afaqs.com/marketing/how-wakefit-spins-zany-internet-antics-into-mattress-marketing-gold-8568186)).

Sounding like AI is now a named failure. Sanchez: "2024 was the year of how can we use AI and 2025 is now how do we not sound like AI or look like AI". And: "If the Motion data showed me that AI content worked better, I would be doing the AI content, but it's just not the case" ([Motion: Savannah Sanchez](https://motionapp.com/library/expert/savannah-sanchez/)). Wikipedia's editors keep the most complete list of AI-writing tells. It includes "delve", "testament", "pivotal", "crucial", "showcase", "vibrant", "robust", "seamless"-style promotional words, "not just X, but Y", stacked triplets, trailing "-ing" clauses and vague "experts say" attributions ([Wikipedia: Signs of AI writing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing)). No measured list of AI-sounding phrases specific to ad scripts was found. The Hindi-side bans in the playbook below (Sanskritised "shuddh", "adbhut", "jaadui", and translated lines like "yeh aapki zindagi badal dega") are DTJ's own inference from the same principle: ban any word a person would not say aloud to a friend.

## Retention is re-earned every five to fifteen seconds

After the hook, the drop-off curve shows which line failed. One 2026 vendor analysis names three shapes ([AdSights](https://www.adsights.ai/resources/guides/video-creative-metrics-benchmarks-2026-guide)). A cliff at 3-4 seconds means the hook resolved but the body never started, so rewrite the bridge line, not the hook. A slow bleed from 5 to 15 seconds means the pacing has no pattern interrupts. A cliff at 14-15 seconds means the offer or CTA framing broke trust. The long-standing practitioner rule is to give a new reason to keep watching **every 5-15 seconds** ([UGC Social, older 2023](https://ugcsocial.substack.com/p/hook-stacking-the-secret-to-longer)). Denney explains why open loops work: "Our brains crave resolution" ([Motion: Dara Denney](https://motionapp.com/library/expert/dara-denney/)).

Pacing does not have to mean fast cutting. Blue Tea's median is only **2.45 cuts per 10 seconds (1.4-5.8)**. Presenter reels sit at 0.5-2.3 cuts per 10 seconds, while montage and animated reels run 6-11 (**DTJ's own analysis**, [style.json](file:///D:/CODE%20CONTAINERR/WEB%20DEV/dtj-reel-engine/docs/ugc-reels/libraries/blue-tea/style.json); [breakdown #1-13](file:///D:/CODE%20CONTAINERR/WEB%20DEV/dtj-reel-engine/docs/ugc-reels/libraries/blue-tea/breakdown_01_13.md)). The slow presenter reels change *something on screen* every 1-2 seconds instead: a slam title, a picture-in-picture card, a 3D insert, a struck-through label, a new location for each chapter. The founder warehouse Q&A (#17) is almost one continuous take, yet keyword slams, photo cards and animated question marks keep it moving. The 14-day timeline (#9/#20) moves to a new room and outfit for each day. Blue Tea's slam titles also use colour as a code that viewers learn: **red for the problem, green for the good news, yellow for proof** ([DTJ Senso playbook](file:///D:/CODE%20CONTAINERR/WEB%20DEV/dtj-reel-engine/docs/ugc-reels/brands/senso/blue-tea-playbook.md)).

Length is a trade-off, not a rule. For cold 15-30 second ads, secondary benchmarks put the 15-second hold (15-second plays divided by 3-second plays) near **20%**, falling to about **12% for 30-60 second ads**. They also report that longer videos lose about half of cold viewers before 15 seconds ([AdSights citing Triple Whale and others](https://www.adsights.ai/resources/guides/video-creative-metrics-benchmarks-2026-guide)). Sanchez's rule of thumb is that ad length in seconds should match the audience's age, so 18-34-year-olds get 18-34 seconds with quick cuts, and 40-60-year-olds "have money and they have time" ([Motion: Savannah Sanchez](https://motionapp.com/library/expert/savannah-sanchez/)). TikTok's older model found 21-34 seconds gave the largest conversion lift, but it dates from 2021 ([TikTok, 2021](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/en-US/blog/creative-that-drives-conversions)). Hott's counterweight is "Make the longest ad that you can that doesn't require any edits" ([Alex Cooper](https://alexcooper.beehiiv.com/p/7-facebook-advertising-lessons-i-learned-from-barry-hott)). Blue Tea runs a **36-second median (29-51)** next to three **5-second bumpers** cut from one model shoot (#22-#24). DTJ should read that as a portfolio: a long explainer for people still deciding and a bumper as a reminder.

Captions are not optional in Indian feeds, where many people watch with the sound off. TikTok's only official caption-pace number is **5-10 on-screen words per second** ([TikTok Ads Help](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/creative-best-practices)). One guide reports that Facebook Reels ads do not get automatic captions, so captions should be burned in ([AdLibrary](https://adlibrary.com/posts/meta-ads-creative-best-practices)). Blue Tea's style is **1-4-word chunks on a rounded pill at lower centre, highlighted word by word**. Hindi speech is captioned in English translation or Roman Hindi, never in Devanagari script (**DTJ's own analysis**, [Senso playbook](file:///D:/CODE%20CONTAINERR/WEB%20DEV/dtj-reel-engine/docs/ugc-reels/brands/senso/blue-tea-playbook.md)). For placement, the widely repeated Meta Reels safe zone keeps text out of the **top 14%, the bottom 35% and 6% at each side**. The bottom margin grows to 40% when a disclaimer is shown. These figures are secondary and should be checked against Meta's own template ([Kive](https://kive.ai/learn/meta-ad-sizes); [AdNabu](https://blog.adnabu.com/meta-ads/meta-safe-zones/)). DTJ's house caption mark at about 69% down the frame sits at or just inside that 65% line, so check it against the official Ads Manager template before locking it.

## Proof answers one objection, and the CTA asks twice

Proof works when it is specific and aimed at one doubt. Runneth (Motion) advises specific details over generic praise and warns that implausible results and over-eager delivery cost trust ([Runneth](https://runneth.com/blog/how-to-create-effective-ugc-ads)). SideShift's testimonial template adds **one minor honest drawback** to make the rest believable. Its objection-handling template answers price, then use case, then "what's the catch" ([SideShift](https://sideshift.app/blog/ugc-script-template)). Social proof only earns budget when it answers a named objection and supports a clear angle ([Selzee](https://selzee.com/blog/social-proof-ads)).

Blue Tea's proof devices are a menu DTJ can reuse with its clients' real facts (**DTJ's own analysis**):

| Proof device | Blue Tea reel | Objection it answers |
|---|---|---|
| Tearing the tea bag open into a bowl, each ingredient raw in hand with its own title | #6 (121 days) | "What is actually inside?" |
| Live colour change (hot water turns it blue, lemon turns it purple) | #4, #14, #18 | "Is this real or a gimmick?" |
| The founder packing orders in a warehouse full of cartons | #17, #19 | "Is this a real company? Do people buy it?" |
| A sceptical podcast host saying "I thought it was a scam" | #11/#25 | "Is this a scam?" |
| A customer count spoken in the hook | #1 ("25 lakh"), #12 ("17 lakh") | "Does anyone use it?" |
| "Pair it with healthy diet and exercise... eventually" | #18 | "Is this too good to be true?" |

Sources: [DTJ breakdown #1-13](file:///D:/CODE%20CONTAINERR/WEB%20DEV/dtj-reel-engine/docs/ugc-reels/libraries/blue-tea/breakdown_01_13.md); [DTJ breakdown #14-25](file:///D:/CODE%20CONTAINERR/WEB%20DEV/dtj-reel-engine/docs/ugc-reels/libraries/blue-tea/breakdown_14_25.md). One warning shows up in Blue Tea's own library: two live ads give **two different customer counts (25 lakh and 17 lakh)**. A viewer who sees both may stop believing either. For DTJ, every number in every variant must come from one dated, sourced figure.

Indian trust data points the same way as these devices. In a Rukam Capital and YouGov survey of 5,000+ people across 18 states (January 2026), **celebrity endorsement influenced about 3%** of buyers, against 23% who trust creator recommendations. In Tier 3, 37% start with YouTube reviews and 43% check the brand's own website before buying ([Storyboard18](https://www.storyboard18.com/brand-marketing/37-of-tier-3-consumers-rely-on-youtube-reviews-celebrity-influence-drops-to-3-report-87756.htm)). In Meta's commissioned festive study, **two-thirds say brands that work with credible creators earn their trust** ([Meta Newsroom, Sep 2025](https://about.fb.com/news/2025/09/genai-creators-and-reels-are-reshaping-festive-shopping-in-india/)). Blue Tea's longest-running ad (#1, 154 days) opens by boasting that it has **no celebrity, no TV ad and no film star**. That hook fits what the survey says Indian buyers believe. Cash on delivery (COD) as a first-order "trust bridge" is practitioner opinion only, with no measured A/B data inside ad creative ([eChai Ventures](https://echai.ventures/d2c/d2c-cro-store-design/trust-signals-for-new-unknown-indian-brand)). Say "COD available" only if it is true, and treat it as a hypothesis to test.

On CTAs, the best data is Billo's six months of creator ads (sample size not disclosed). **74% of top performers used a hard CTA**, and all five highest-CTR ads did. Concrete offers (a stated discount or deadline) beat vague pressure such as "they sell out fast". **Six of the top 10 had a second CTA earlier in the video**, and the top ad used a mid-video line on hesitation before the offer at the close ([Billo Creative Insider](https://billo.app/creative-insider/inspiration-ideas/cta-examples/)). Runneth argues for a friendly, recommendation-style delivery ([Runneth](https://runneth.com/blog/how-to-create-effective-ugc-ads)). The two fit together: make the ask clear and direct, say it in the speaker's own voice, and say it twice. Blue Tea's CTAs are habit instructions ("din mein teen baar", "kabhi skip mat karna", "aaj hi switch karo"), a "comment 'link'" prompt (#14), or an animated BUY NOW button with a cursor click. It keeps prices out of the video entirely. Its "Flat 30% Off" appears only in the ad caption of #21 (**DTJ's own analysis**, [breakdown #14-25](file:///D:/CODE%20CONTAINERR/WEB%20DEV/dtj-reel-engine/docs/ugc-reels/libraries/blue-tea/breakdown_14_25.md)). That is a sensible split for DTJ too. Offers change weekly, so keeping them in the caption avoids re-rendering video and stops an expired offer living on inside a reel.

## Meta now counts concepts, so a batch has to look different

On 16 December 2025, Meta put in writing what practitioners had suspected. Its system **"clusters" ads that share key visual and thematic attributes and treats them as variations of one creative**. Iteration (small changes such as text CTAs on identical visuals) "should not replace true creative diversification". Ads should be "truly different in look, feel, storyline, and message" across format, persona or motivation, creator and asset type ([Meta, Demystifying Creative Diversification](https://www.facebook.com/business/news/demystifying-creative-diversification?locale=en_US)). Andromeda, the retrieval stage that narrows tens of millions of candidate ads to a few thousand per person, launched in December 2024 with a reported **+8% ads quality** on selected segments ([Meta Engineering](https://engineering.fb.com/2024/12/02/production-engineering/meta-andromeda-advantage-automation-next-gen-personalized-ads-retrieval-engine/)). TikTok's official advice is close: **3-5 creatives per ad group, 3-5 diversified ad groups per campaign**, "creatives with big differences" when testing ([TikTok Ads Help](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/creative-best-practices)). Its Shop guidance adds a new creative **about once a week** ([TikTok Shop best practices](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/best-practices-for-video-shopping-ads-with-tiktok-shop)).

That changes how the "hooks x bodies" grid should be used. The classic method still works for *iteration*: on a body that has shown early signal, run **3-5 hooks of different types with everything after second 3 identical**, and judge them in stages (hook rate, then hold rate, then cost per purchase). A hook that wins attention but loses sales over-promises against its body ([SideShift](https://sideshift.app/blog/ugc-script-template); [Kalungi](https://www.kalungi.com/blog/test-ad-hooks-not-full-ads)). The hooks must differ in type, not just wording ([MakeInfluence](https://www.makeinfluence.com/en/academy/ugc-hooks-for-ecommerce-ads)). But if only the spoken line changes over the same footage, Meta will likely treat the result as one ad. Billo's own guide admits that 20 hooks on the same shot and pace still read as one ad ([Billo](https://billo.app/blog/ad-hooks-variations/)). When the goal is reaching new pockets of the audience, change the opening *visual* too: a new setting, framing or pattern interrupt. Volume still matters, because winners are rare. In Motion's data **about 5% of ads become winners and about half get almost no spend** ([Motion / Runneth](https://runneth.com/thumbstop-pulse/creative-benchmarks-2026/)).

Blue Tea's live library is close to a working example of Meta's advice (**DTJ's own analysis**). At the time of collection it was running 21 different videos in about 15 format families at once: founder talking head, warehouse Q&A, podcast, 3D characters, an AI villain, a meme glow-up, a creator wow moment, a VO montage and 5-second bumpers. It mixed Hindi, Hinglish and English, male and female presenters, and live action, AI and animation. It also reuses smartly: three bumpers with three messages from one model shoot (#22-#24), and the same ad caption on two quite different videos (#18, #19) ([DTJ Senso playbook](file:///D:/CODE%20CONTAINERR/WEB%20DEV/dtj-reel-engine/docs/ugc-reels/brands/senso/blue-tea-playbook.md)). Meta's own measured format result supports making all of them native vertical. Reels ads with **9:16 video, audio and key elements inside the safe zone had a 34.5% lower cost per result than image ads on Reels**, in a Meta-run meta-analysis of 15 split tests ([Meta for Business: Reels ads](https://www.facebook.com/business/ads/facebook-instagram-reels-ads), via search summary). For brands that can get a real creator to post, Meta-supplied lift studies report a **45% lower median cost per incremental purchase** for partnership ads. That figure comes from 20 EMEA studies at 92% confidence, so it is company-supplied and below the usual 95% bar ([Relevant Audience](https://www.relevantaudience.com/meta/meta-partnership-ads-30-percent-45-lower-incremental-cost/)).

## Indian buyers trust creators, everyday Hinglish and real deals

Creator supply and engagement in India lean Hindi and regional. Kofluence's 2025 report (about 1,000 creators, self-reported) finds **68.2% of creators work mainly in Hindi and 23.9% in regional languages**. Tier 3/4 creators earn **4.5-5.5% engagement against 3-4% in metros**, and over 62% get more vernacular briefs than before ([NetInfluencer on Kofluence](https://www.netinfluencer.com/india-influencer-marketing-industry-estimated-at-400m-as-regional-creators-reshape-brand-strategy/)). A MICA researcher describes Hinglish as the bridge brands ask for because it reaches Hindi and English speakers in metros and small towns alike ([MICA repository](https://ir.mica.ac.in/entities/publication/17b86fb2-f287-431a-82cb-8fd7abadb2d2/full)). For Gujarat, the only specific guidance found is older. A 2020 practitioner piece suggests about **80% Gujarati and 10-15% Hindi for a Gujarat-only spice brand**, Gujarati for FMCG aimed at women over 45, and English-led copy for an Ahmedabad urban pilot ([Social Samosa, 2020](https://www.socialsamosa.com/2020/01/state-of-social-media-marketing-in-gujarat)). The often-quoted "vernacular gets 2x engagement" line is a trade claim with no primary source ([exchange4media](https://www.exchange4media.com/advertising-news/from-challenge-to-opportunity-vernacular-advertising-sees-2x-growth-in-engagement-rates-141481.html)). No Meta or Google report on conversion by language in India was found. The defaults below are a starting position to test, not a proven answer:

| Audience | Voiceover | Captions |
|---|---|---|
| Pan-India mass, Tier 2/3 | Hindi or Hinglish, English product words | Roman Hindi or English (Blue Tea's choice) |
| Gujarat household, food, FMCG, women 45+ | Gujarati, English product words | Roman or Gujarati script (untested; worth an A/B) |
| Premium metro | English or English-led Hinglish | English |

Festive and seasonal moments are wider than Diwali. Meta's 2025 festive playbook describes a season running **from July-August to New Year's Eve**. It tells advertisers to lead with percent-off sales, bundles, flash deals and bank or EMI offers, and reports that **78% of festive shopping conversations happen on Meta platforms, especially WhatsApp**. Gen Z (18-24) is the most price- and code-sensitive group ([Meta Newsroom](https://about.fb.com/news/2025/09/genai-creators-and-reels-are-reshaping-festive-shopping-in-india/)). Blue Tea uses moments as hooks rather than offers. It runs three separate wedding-season reels (#17, #21, #22) and an April Fools' prank (#8) (**DTJ's own analysis**). A moment hook with a real, concrete offer in the caption combines both lessons.

Humour works in India when it carries one product insight and stays away from sensitive targets. Wakefit's CMO bans political, religious and personal humour, tests with neutral groups, and rejects **about 30 ideas for every 3 released**. Its "Gaddagiri" films, a parody of the 1992 film *Dadagiri*, earned over 1 million organic interactions on the single insight that bad sleep explains bad behaviour ([afaqs](https://www.afaqs.com/marketing/how-wakefit-spins-zany-internet-antics-into-mattress-marketing-gold-8568186)). The counter-example is Bombay Shaving Company's newspaper ad about a real school topper, which drew wide criticism, a response from ASCI and a rival's parody ([afaqs](https://www.afaqs.com/news/advertising/caresmith-india-takes-a-jab-at-bombay-shaving-companys-controversial-ad)). Blue Tea's filmy courtroom ("Order, order!", "My Lord", a "Three weeks later" meme card, #16) is the safe kind. Its "Tera kuch nahi hoga" meme, which shows a heavier woman eating at a party as the "before" (#7), is the risky kind. It mocks a body and runs into Meta's rule against creating negative self-perception (next section).

Four Indian brands have sourced creative detail. **The Whole Truth's** paid ads are more offer-led than its brand image: of 198 ads analysed, 81% were bottom-funnel, 68% static, and 79% said "Order now", built around one checkable claim ("Pure Protein. No Fillers.") plus rupee discounts ([Hawky](https://hawky.ai/blog/decoding-the-whole-truth)). **Two Brothers Organic Farms** sells through real farmers' faces and real processes ("Know Your Farmer") ([Indian Retailer](https://indianretailer.com/interview/retail-people/new-commerce/two-brothers-organic-farms-targets-100-percent-growth-eyes)). **Wakefit** shows topical, filmy Hinglish humour. **Blue Tea** shows the full performance toolkit analysed throughout this report. One trust shortcut is a trap. Sony Pictures Networks India sent legal notices and had ads using **Shark Tank India clips** taken down, and one founder had to stop all ads within 15 days of the season ending ([YourStory](https://ts.yourstory.com/2024/05/beyond-shark-tank-india-mintree-allter-quirksmith-turms-d2c-brands)). Do not use show footage or logos in paid ads.

## Blue Tea's longest runners break rules DTJ has to keep

The ads Blue Tea has run longest are also its riskiest. What a library shows to be working is not the same as what is allowed, and several of these ads were live after the rules they break came into force. The relevant rules, in order of how often they bite a food, tea or wellness reel, are below. This is research, not legal advice.

**ASCI on creators and AI people.** Any "material connection" (money, free product, discount, family or employment) needs an upfront label such as "Ad", "Collaboration" or "Partnership", superimposed on the video. A caption hashtag or Instagram's "Paid Partnership" tag on its own is not enough. The label stays on screen **for at least 3 seconds on videos of 15 seconds or less, and for one third of the length on videos up to 2 minutes**, so about 10 seconds on a 30-second reel. Computer-generated "virtual influencers" must also disclose that viewers are "not interacting with a real human being". Health claims of prevention, treatment or cure must come from a qualified person whose qualification is shown at the start ([ASCI Influencer Guidelines](https://www.ascionline.in/social/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/ASCI-Influencer-Guidelines.pdf)). ASCI's **Guidelines for Responsible Labelling of Synthetically Generated Content** (signed 17 September 2026) go further. **Even with a label, they ban** fabricated testimonials, visuals that exaggerate results (ASCI's own example is an AI before/after from 100 kg to 70 kg), fake locations shown as real, and deepfakes. They **require a label** ("Video created using AI" or "enhanced using AI") on synthetic influencers and ambassadors, on replicated real faces or voices, and on fabricated situations that affect how viewers understand product performance. They need **no label** for routine edits, decorative backgrounds, obviously fantastical effects, AI-written copy or subtitles. The rules take effect three months after publication, which is **about 29 December 2026** ([Guidelines text](https://foxmandal.in/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/Guidelines-for-Responsible-Labelling-of-Synthetically-Generated-Content-in-Advertising.pdf); [Khaitan note](https://www.khaitanco.com/sites/default/files/2026-10/Ergo_ASCI%20Guidelines%20on%20SGC%20Labelling_12%2010%202026.pdf)). One summary says 1 January 2027 instead ([Sanskriti IAS](https://www.sanskritiias.com/current-affairs/asci-releases-ai-advertising-guidelines-2026-key-facts-need-for-guidelines-four-key-areas-ai-labelli)). Either way, DTJ should comply now.

**FSSAI on food words.** "Natural" means a single unprocessed food with nothing added. A blend may say "made from natural ingredients" only if every ingredient qualifies. "Fresh" cannot describe packed, processed food. "Pure" means a single ingredient free of contamination. "Traditional" needs a recipe at least 30 years old ([Food Safety Works](https://foodsafetyworks.com/insights/conditions-behind-the-conditional-claims/); [FSSAI press note](https://fssai.gov.in/upload/media/FSSAI_News_NaturalFood_Economic_14_11_2018.pdf)). A **28 May 2025 advisory told food businesses to stop using "100%"** on labels, packs and promotions, citing "100% natural" and "100% pure". Amway, Emami, Bonn and others withdrew such claims, and FSSAI issued a prohibition order on some Dabur products ([Storyboard18](https://www.storyboard18.com/how-it-works/fssai-cracks-down-on-misleading-100-natural-claims-in-food-advertising-67782.htm); [Moneylife](https://www.moneylife.in/article/fssai-bars-sale-of-daburs-100-percentage-claim-products-alleges-repeated-noncompliance-with-advertising-norms/81249.html)). Nutrition claims must meet the regulation's fixed thresholds, for example **at least 6 g fibre per 100 g for "high in dietary fibre"** ([FSSAI digest](https://fssai-digest.fssai.gov.in/research-and-innovations/claims.php)). The exact thresholds for "low sugar" and "sugar free" were not verified, so do not script those claims until someone checks the gazette text. The ad must never claim more than the pack label can.

**The Drugs and Magic Remedies Act and CCPA.** Section 3 of the 1954 Act bans ads that suggest a product for "the diagnosis, cure, mitigation, treatment or prevention" of scheduled conditions, which include **obesity and diabetes**. It also bans claims about menstrual disorders and sexual capacity. A first offence can bring up to 6 months in prison ([India Code](https://www.indiacode.nic.in/indiacode/bitstream/123456789/1412/1/A1954-21.pdf); [Wikipedia summary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drugs_and_Magic_Remedies_(Objectionable_Advertisements)_Act,_1954)). The Supreme Court's 2024 action against Patanjali's medicine ads shows these rules are enforced ([WION](https://www.wionews.com/india-news/indias-top-court-slaps-ban-on-patanjali-medicine-ads-for-misleading-claims-694430/amp)). Under the CCPA's 2022 guidelines, endorsements must reflect a "genuine, reasonably current opinion", and disclaimers cannot contradict the main claim. Penalties reach **₹10 lakh, or ₹50 lakh for repeat offences**, and endorsers can be barred for up to 3 years ([Khaitan & Co](https://khaitanco.com/thought-leaderships/CCPA-releases-guidelines-for-Prevention-of-Misleading-Advertisements-and-Endorsements-for-Misleading-Advertisements-2022)). The 2023 dark-pattern rules ban **false urgency** such as "Only 2 left!" when it is untrue ([JSA](https://www.jsalaw.com/newsletters-and-updates/ccpa-issues-guidelines-for-prevention-and-regulation-of-dark-patterns-2023/)).

**Meta's health and personal-attribute rules.** Meta's ad rules say ads may not "assert or imply" knowledge of a viewer's medical, physical or mental condition. In practice that rules out "you" plus a condition, such as "Are you overweight?" ([Meta Personal Attributes](https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/objectionable-content/privacy-violations-personal-attributes)). Weight-loss products and supplements must target 18+. Ads may not create negative self-perception. Weight-loss before/after comparisons and pinched-fat close-ups are not allowed. Sources disagree on cosmetic before/after, so treat it as 18+ only. Since early 2025, brands tied to health conditions face limits on optimising for purchases ([Accelerated Digital Media 2026 guide](https://www.accelerateddigitalmedia.com/insights/guide-to-social-media-health-ad-restrictions-2026/)). Meta's Transparency Center pages would not render for direct reading in this research, so their exact current wording needs a manual check. DTJ's inference is that a tea or spice brand counts as general food until its own copy talks about conditions. Health-condition copy can push it into the restricted group and weaken purchase optimisation.

Set against those rules, Blue Tea's library reads as a list of things not to copy (**DTJ's own analysis**):

| Blue Tea device | Reel (days) | Rule it runs into | DTJ version |
|---|---|---|---|
| "Charbi ko khatam karein jad se" (end fat from the root) | #2/#5 (152) | DMR Act schedule (obesity); Meta weight-loss rules | Ingredient characters that each say a cleared, non-disease job |
| AI woman with an 81 -> 59 kg counter; customer before/after photos | #12 (104) | ASCI ban on AI-exaggerated results; Meta weight-loss before/after | Experience timeline with no body outcome |
| "100% natural", "100% Ayurvedic ingredients" | #6, #7, #19, #21 | FSSAI May 2025 "100%" advisory; "natural" conditions | "Just [named ingredients], nothing added", only if literally true |
| "Kuch khaate hi pet gubbare jaisa phool jaata hai?" | #10 (107) | Meta personal attributes ("you" + condition) | Situational, third person: "Shaadi ka khaana, teen plate, phir woh bhaari feeling" |
| AI re-enactments of a customer's story | #13 (104) | ASCI label for fabricated situations; fabricated testimonial if the customer is not real | Real customers only, with consent and proof; AI scenes labelled |
| "Periods ko banao easy" | #24 (44) | DMR Act Section 3 (menstrual disorders) | No period-related benefit claims |
| Heavier woman eating as the "before" in a taunt meme | #7 (121) | Meta negative self-perception | Meme glow-up about a habit (burnt chai pans), never a body |

The Senso playbook already applies most of this correctly. It bans weight-loss, fat, detox, cure and immunity promises, invented customers and AI founders ([DTJ Senso playbook](file:///D:/CODE%20CONTAINERR/WEB%20DEV/dtj-reel-engine/docs/ugc-reels/brands/senso/blue-tea-playbook.md)). One line still needs changing. It names "Kuch khaate hi pet phool jaata hai?" as the model "real question" and reuses that opener in format G. That is the "you plus a condition" pattern Meta warns against. Replace it with a situational line before any format G reel ships. The formats that carry over cleanly are those whose engine is the mechanic, not the claim: 3D ingredient characters (#2/#5, 152 days), the villain who begs (#3, 152 days), the fake-bad-news prank (#8, 121 days), the courtroom and craving-monster cartoons (#15, #16, 77 days) and the tear-open ingredient breakdown (#6, 121 days). Cartoon characters that are obviously not real people fit ASCI's "obviously fantastical" no-label category. That is DTJ's inference from the guideline text and should be confirmed. Labelling anyway costs little.

| 6 | Word budget | Total words within about 2.8 x length in seconds (plus or minus 15%) |
| 7 | Spoken register | Read aloud; no line you would not say to a friend; no banned AI or Sanskritised words |
| 8 | Questions and "you" | About one real question per 30 s; "you" used, never with a health or body condition |
| 9 | Screen change | Something new on screen every 1-2 s; a new reason to watch every 5-15 s |
| 10 | Bridge | The line after the hook pays part of the hook and opens a second loop |
| 11 | Captions | Burned in, 1-4-word chunks, inside the safe zone, language and script chosen for the audience |
| 12 | Proof | Every proof is shown, answers one named objection and comes from the client's file |
| 13 | Numbers | Every number traces to one dated source; the same figure in every variant |
| 14 | CTA | One soft mention mid-video, one specific ask at the end, spoken and on screen |
| 15 | Offers and urgency | Any offer, "free" or deadline is real and stated with its condition |
| 16 | Claim words | No "100%"; "natural", "pure", "fresh", "traditional", "original" only where the FSSAI meaning fits |
| 17 | Disease and body | No cure, treat, prevent, weight, fat, sugar control, period, immunity or detox outcome |
| 18 | Before/after | No body before/after; no AI image that exaggerates a result |
| 19 | AI people | AI characters present, demonstrate or act a cartoon role; none poses as a customer or founder |

## Conclusion

The research changes what "copy the winners" should mean for DTJ. Blue Tea proves that Indian buyers stay for fast Hindi-first hooks, a named enemy, one ingredient per job and a drink-in-a-glass hero shot. The same library proves that a category leader can run claims for months that FSSAI, the Drugs and Magic Remedies Act, Meta and now ASCI treat as off-limits. The safe opportunity is mechanics without claims. Three of Blue Tea's longest-running ideas (the ingredient characters, the begging villain and the fake bad news, at 121-152 days) get their pull from the device, not the promise. The cartoon formats also fall closest to ASCI's "obviously fantastical" category. From about 29 December 2026, the most common AI-UGC format, a synthetic "customer" describing results, is banned even with a label. AI reels built as characters, demonstrations and labelled presenters are both the compliant route and, on Blue Tea's own evidence, a route that can run for a long time.

Two open questions are worth DTJ answering with its own data, because nobody has published them for India. The first is language. No source measures conversion by Hindi, Hinglish, Gujarati or English, or by Roman against native-script captions. One concept rendered in three languages, with only the language changed, would give DTJ an answer no competitor can cite. The second is the hook versus brand-first conflict in Meta's guidance, which stays unresolved. Opening on the hook with the product already in frame satisfies both sides until DTJ's own hook-rate and hold-rate data, judged against each account's average rather than borrowed benchmarks, settles it. One input gap remains. DTJ's breakdown of reel #25 and the pattern summary for #14-#25 are unfinished in the source file, so the Blue Tea patterns above rest mainly on reels #1-#24.
