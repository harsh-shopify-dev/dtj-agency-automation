# Advertising-claim rules for Indian food, beverage, spice, wellness and personal-care video ads (India + Meta), 2024-2026

Research date: 22-23 Oct 2026. This is research, not legal advice. Several primary pages (fssai.gov.in, Meta Transparency Center) would not load from this session (TLS errors / JavaScript-only pages), so some points rest on law-firm or trade summaries. Each point is marked with its source, and the gaps are listed. Two primary texts WERE read in full: the ASCI Influencer Guidelines PDF (with Addendum 2, 17 Aug 2023) and the ASCI Guidelines for Responsible Labelling of Synthetically Generated Content (Sept 2026).

## Q1. ASCI Code and guidelines: misleading claims, substantiation, testimonials, influencers, virtual/AI people, Ayurveda/health

### Takeaway
ASCI is self-regulatory but acts fast on social ads. Every claim (and every "my experience" testimonial) must be backed by evidence the brand holds before the ad runs. Influencer and creator ads need an upfront "Ad"/"Collaboration" type label, kept on screen for a set time. Health or nutrition "advice" needs a qualified person who shows their qualification at the start. NEW in 2026: AI-generated people, influencers and fabricated scenes need a label ("Video created using AI"), and fake AI testimonials or AI-exaggerated results are banned even with a label. These rules take effect about 3 months after publication, so around end Dec 2026.

### Cited Findings
**Influencer / creator disclosure (ASCI Influencer Guidelines; PDF re-uploaded April 2025, original 2021, Addendum 2 dated 17 Aug 2023)**
- A label is needed whenever there is a "material connection". That covers money, free products (even unsolicited), discounts, gifts, trips, barter, or a family or employment relationship. — [ASCI Influencer Guidelines PDF](https://www.ascionline.in/social/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/ASCI-Influencer-Guidelines.pdf)
- A label is required even if the creator's review is "unbiased or fully originated by Influencer". No label is needed only when there is no connection at all, e.g. someone bought the product and likes it. — [ASCI PDF](https://www.ascionline.in/social/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/ASCI-Influencer-Guidelines.pdf)
- Placement: the label must be "hard to miss". It is not enough in the bio, at the end of the video, behind "more", or buried in hashtags. The platform's own tool (Instagram "Paid Partnership") counts only "in addition to" the creator's own label. For picture or video posts without text, the label must be superimposed on the video. — [ASCI PDF](https://www.ascionline.in/social/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/ASCI-Influencer-Guidelines.pdf)
- Time on screen: videos of 15 s or less need the label for at least 3 s. Videos over 15 s and under 2 min need it for one third of the length. Videos of 2 min or more need it for the whole section where the brand is mentioned. Live streams need it at the start and the end. — [ASCI PDF](https://www.ascionline.in/social/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/ASCI-Influencer-Guidelines.pdf)
- Allowed labels: Advertisement, Ad, Sponsored, Collaboration, Partnership, Employee, Free gift, Affiliate, the Instagram "Paid Partnership" tag, and the YouTube "Includes Paid Promotion" tag. The label can be in English or in the ad's own language. — [ASCI PDF](https://www.ascionline.in/social/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/ASCI-Influencer-Guidelines.pdf)
- Virtual influencers (computer-generated "people" with human features) "must additionally disclose" that consumers are "not interacting with a real human being". — [ASCI PDF, clause 1.4](https://www.ascionline.in/social/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/ASCI-Influencer-Guidelines.pdf)
- Responsibility is shared. The brand must make sure the content follows the ASCI Code and can ask for edits or deletion; the creator makes the disclosure. Influencers are told to satisfy themselves that the advertiser can substantiate its claims. — [ASCI PDF, clause 1.5 and Due Diligence](https://www.ascionline.in/social/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/ASCI-Influencer-Guidelines.pdf)
- Addendum 2 (17 Aug 2023): for health and nutrition, any "prevention, treatment, cure, remedy for medical conditions" claim must come from a qualified person (medical degree, certified nurse, nutritionist, dietician, physiotherapist, psychologist, etc.). The qualification must appear superimposed upfront or as the opening remark in a video. Unqualified creators may share only "generic information", e.g. a chef promoting a health-food meal service. — [ASCI PDF, Addendum 2](https://www.ascionline.in/social/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/ASCI-Influencer-Guidelines.pdf)

**AI / synthetic people: ASCI "Guidelines for Responsible Labelling of Synthetically Generated Content in Advertising" (final; signed 17 Sept 2026, reported released 29 Sept 2026; draft May 2026)**
- Scope: any audio or visual made or "materially altered" by computer so it appears real, and portrays a person or event likely to be believed real. — [Guidelines text (Fox Mandal-hosted PDF)](https://foxmandal.in/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/Guidelines-for-Responsible-Labelling-of-Synthetically-Generated-Content-in-Advertising.pdf)
- Prohibited even with a label: (A) fabricated endorsements or testimonials that falsely show a real person used or recommended the product; (B) exaggerated results through visuals, e.g. an AI before/after of 100 kg to 70 kg that does not show substantiated results; (C) fake locations shown as real; (D) deepfakes, unauthorised copyrighted work, or a person's likeness without consent. — [Guidelines PDF](https://foxmandal.in/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/Guidelines-for-Responsible-Labelling-of-Synthetically-Generated-Content-in-Advertising.pdf)
- Label mandatory for: (A) "synthetically generated influencers and ambassadors"; (B) a replicated real face or voice, even with consent; (C) fabricated events, settings or situations that may affect understanding of product performance; (D) a product that does not exist yet; (E) synthetic sounds central to the product; (F) sponsored AI chatbot recommendations ("Sponsored by [Brand]"). — [Guidelines PDF](https://foxmandal.in/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/Guidelines-for-Responsible-Labelling-of-Synthetically-Generated-Content-in-Advertising.pdf)
- No label needed for: routine edits and colour correction; decorative backgrounds and ambient music; obviously fantastical effects; AI-written copy; subtitles, translations and accessibility features. — [Guidelines PDF](https://foxmandal.in/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/Guidelines-for-Responsible-Labelling-of-Synthetically-Generated-Content-in-Advertising.pdf)
- Suggested labels: "Audio/Video created using AI" or "Audio/Video enhanced using AI", or the ad platform's own label. A label does not cure an ad that misleads anyway. — [Guidelines PDF](https://foxmandal.in/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/Guidelines-for-Responsible-Labelling-of-Synthetically-Generated-Content-in-Advertising.pdf)
- Effective "on the expiration of 3 months from the date of their publication", i.e. about 29 Dec 2026 if published 29 Sept. — [Guidelines PDF](https://foxmandal.in/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/Guidelines-for-Responsible-Labelling-of-Synthetically-Generated-Content-in-Advertising.pdf); [exchange4media, Sept 2026](https://www.exchange4media.com/advertising-news/asci-issues-guidelines-on-responsible-labelling-of-ai-generated-ad-content-158710.html); [Khaitan ERGO note, 12 Oct 2026](https://www.khaitanco.com/sites/default/files/2026-10/Ergo_ASCI%20Guidelines%20on%20SGC%20Labelling_12%2010%202026.pdf)
- Changes from the May 2026 draft: "AI-generated" became "synthetically generated content", the risk tiers became prohibited / label / no label, and the draft's fictional "AI doctor" example was dropped from the prohibited list. — [search summary of foxmandal.in note](https://foxmandal.in/?p=60219); [ASCI draft PDF](https://www.ascionline.in/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/AI-Draft-Guidelines-1.pdf)
- One summary says final rules are effective "1 January 2027"; others say 3 months from publication. The guideline text itself says 3 months. — [Sanskriti IAS summary](https://www.sanskritiias.com/current-affairs/asci-releases-ai-advertising-guidelines-2026-key-facts-need-for-guidelines-four-key-areas-ai-labelli) (conflict noted)

**Substantiation, testimonials, celebrities, Ayurveda (from ASCI complaint decisions)**
- ASCI's complaints council (CCC) upholds complaints when a celebrity's or user's "personal experience" efficacy claim is not backed by evidence that the person agrees with the claim, or by testimonials. — [ASCI CCC recommendations, Oct-Nov 2018](https://www.ascionline.in/wp-content/uploads/2022/09/asci_oct_nov18_ccc_recom.pdf); [exchange4media summary](https://www.exchange4media.com/advertising-news/asci-upholds-complaints-against-114-out-of-193-advertisements-86891.html)
- Health claims need clinical evidence on a "statistically significant and representative sample". A diabetes "cure in 3 days" claim was struck down as gross exaggeration. — [exchange4media summary of ASCI decisions](https://www.exchange4media.com/advertising-news/asci-upholds-complaints-against-114-out-of-193-advertisements-86891.html)
- "No. 1" and leadership claims (e.g. "No. 1 Supplement for Men") are struck down without independent data. — [exchange4media](https://www.exchange4media.com/advertising-news/asci-upholds-complaints-against-114-out-of-193-advertisements-86891.html)
- "Ayurveda" framing in food ads (e.g. a milk drink with memory-boost claims) was not substantiated without data or literature. "Totally Ayurvedic" without ingredient evidence was misleading. Mentioning an AYUSH or FSSAI licence was misleading "by implication" that the Ministry approved the efficacy. A chyawanprash claim of pollution protection through immunity was not substantiated. — [ASCI CCC April 2018](https://www.ascionline.in/wp-content/uploads/2022/09/asci-april-18-ccc-recomm.pdf); [ASCI CCC Jan 2018](https://www.ascionline.in/wp-content/uploads/2022/09/asci-jan-18-ccc-recomm.pdf)
- The Ministry of AYUSH has asked ASCI to proactively flag misleading traditional-medicine ads. ASCI reports possible Drugs and Magic Remedies Act breaches to the Ministry. — [IAS Parliament explainer](https://www.iasparliament.com/current-affairs/misleading-ads-for-traditional-medicine)

### Inferences
- For DTJ's AI UGC reels (dtj-ugc-reel), from about 29 Dec 2026: an AI-made "customer" or creator persona very likely falls under "synthetically generated influencers and ambassadors". It needs an on-screen "Video created using AI" label, plus the virtual-influencer "not a real human" disclosure. An AI person saying "I used it and my acidity went away" is very likely a prohibited fabricated testimonial, even with a label. Script AI characters as presenters or demonstrators, never as real customers reporting results.
- AI-made before/after visuals (glowing skin, slimmer body, darker hair) are the exact example ASCI bans unless they match substantiated results.
- Creator-posted (whitelisted or partnership) reels need the Ad/Collaboration label for the time windows above. A 30 s reel needs it for at least 10 s.

### Gaps
- I did not read the full ASCI Code text (Chapter I truthfulness and substantiation, Chapter IV comparative ads) or the separate ASCI guidelines on celebrities, disclaimers and "harmful gender stereotypes". The ascionline.in code page was not fetched.
- I found no ASCI guideline from 2024-2025 specific to "natural" or "Ayurveda" claims in food. The search returned only 2017-2022 decisions. ASCI may have newer sector reports, not verified.
- ASCI has an HFSS / children's food guideline, but its details were not verified in this session.

## Q2. FSSAI (Advertising and Claims) Regulations 2018 and later actions

### Takeaway
For packaged food, tea, spices and ghee, FSSAI governs every claim, and the ad must match the label. Nutrition claims ("high fibre", "low sugar") must meet fixed numbers in the regulation's schedules. "Natural", "fresh", "pure", "traditional", "original" and "authentic" have strict meanings. "100%" has been banned in practice since a May 2025 advisory. No food ad may say it prevents, treats or cures a disease.

### Cited Findings
- Regulations published 19 Nov 2018; compliance from 1 July 2019. — [FSSAI digest](https://fssai-digest.fssai.gov.in/research-and-innovations/claims.php) (via search snippet; page fetch failed with a TLS error); [TÜV SÜD summary](https://www.tuvsud.com/en-in/e-ssentials-newsletter/food-and-health-essentials/e-ssentials-2-2019/regulatory-news-and-updates/india-fssai-modifies-food-advertising-labelling-requirements)
- Claims listed in the schedules with their conditions can be used without prior approval. Any other claim needs FSSAI approval and scientific substantiation. — [FSSAI press coverage, Apr 2018](https://fssai.gov.in/upload/media/5b44745032df7FSSAI_News_Claims_Reg_FNB_03_04_2018.pdf); [law.asia](https://law.asia/food-safety-standards-regulation/) (search snippet)
- Nutrient claims must meet Schedule I thresholds. For example, "High in dietary fibre" needs at least 6 g fibre per 100 g (solids). — [FSSAI digest snippet](https://fssai-digest.fssai.gov.in/research-and-innovations/claims.php)
- Conditional terms:
  - "Natural" (also "real", "genuine") means a single food from a recognised plant, animal, microbe or mineral source, with nothing added and processing that does not change its nature. A composite food may say "made from natural ingredients" only if every ingredient meets this.
  - "Fresh" means not processed beyond washing, peeling, chilling or chopping (irradiation up to 1 kGy allowed). "Freshly packed" is not allowed.
  - "Pure" means a single-ingredient food free from contamination; mixes may say "made with pure ingredients".
  - "Traditional" means the recipe, formulation or process has existed for at least 30 years.
  - "Original" means the recipe is traceable to its roots and unchanged.
  - "Authentic" must be justified with proof.
  — [Food Safety Works analysis](https://foodsafetyworks.com/insights/conditions-behind-the-conditional-claims/); consistent with [FSSAI press note Nov 2018 "Natural food has to be natural"](https://fssai.gov.in/upload/media/FSSAI_News_NaturalFood_Economic_14_11_2018.pdf) and [SS Rana note](https://ssrana.in/articles/india-govt-to-restrict-use-of-terms-like-fresh-and-natural-in-food-ads/)
- If one of these words is in a brand, product name or trademark, the label must carry a disclaimer (at least 3 mm): "This is only a brand name or trade mark and does not represent its true nature". — [Food Safety Works](https://foodsafetyworks.com/insights/conditions-behind-the-conditional-claims/); [TÜV SÜD](https://www.tuvsud.com/en-in/e-ssentials-newsletter/food-and-health-essentials/e-ssentials-2-2019/regulatory-news-and-updates/india-fssai-modifies-food-advertising-labelling-requirements)
- June 2024: FSSAI ordered "100%" claims removed from reconstituted fruit juice labels and ads, with packaging used up by 1 Sept 2024, because the regulations contain no provision for a "100%" claim. — [All India Radio News](https://www.newsonair.gov.in/fssai-issues-directive-mandating-all-fbos-to-remove-any-claim-of-100-fruit-juices-from-labels); [Moneylife](https://moneylife.in/article/fssai-asks-foodmakers-to-remove-100-percentage-fruit-juice-claim-from-ads-packaging-labels/74303.html)
- 28 May 2025 advisory: stop using "100%" on food labels, packaging and promotional content. FSSAI said the term has no defined meaning and may mislead about purity, citing "100% natural" and "100% pure". — [Storyboard18](https://www.storyboard18.com/how-it-works/fssai-cracks-down-on-misleading-100-natural-claims-in-food-advertising-67782.htm); [Aparajitha](https://www.aparajitha.com/fssai-directs-discontinuation-of-claims-on-food-labels-and-all-promotional-materials/)
- Enforcement: Amway India, Emami, Bonn and three others withdrew "100%" claims. FSSAI issued a prohibition order on some Dabur "100%" products. — [Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/news/fssai-says-amway-india-emami-bonn-biscuits-3-other-firms-withdraw-100-claims-on-product-labels-ads); [Moneylife](https://www.moneylife.in/article/fssai-bars-sale-of-daburs-100-percentage-claim-products-alleges-repeated-noncompliance-with-advertising-norms/81249.html)
- The regulations also cover non-addition claims ("no added sugar"), health claims, dietary-guideline claims and conditional claims, and prohibit, for example, presenting a packaged food as a complete meal replacement. — [Food Safety Works / FSSAI coverage via search](https://foodsafetyworks.com/insights/conditions-behind-the-conditional-claims/)

### Inferences
- Ghee: "pure desi ghee" is plausible only if it is single-ingredient and uncontaminated. If "Pure" is part of the brand name, the brand-name disclaimer applies. "100% pure" must go after the May 2025 advisory.
- Spices: "freshly ground" is risky, because packed products cannot be called "fresh". Prefer "ground in small batches" with the real date or process stated, if true. "Traditional recipe" needs 30-plus years of proof.
- Tea: "natural" works only for a plain single tea. A blend may say "made from natural ingredients" only if every ingredient qualifies. Flavoured teas with added flavour likely fail.
- The ad must not claim anything the label cannot carry. If the pack does not say "high fibre", the reel should not either.

### Gaps
- The exact Schedule I numbers for "low sugar", "sugar free", "low fat" and "source of protein" were NOT verified; fssai.gov.in PDFs failed with TLS errors. Do not script these claims until someone checks the gazette text. My unverified memory of the Codex-style values (sugar free ≤0.5 g/100 g; low sugar ≤5 g/100 g) must not be used as a source.
- The regulatory wording that bans "prevent, alleviate, treat or cure" claims, and the Schedule list of permitted reduction-of-disease-risk claims, were not retrieved in primary form. This session found that the general principle exists (from secondary sources and the ASCI/DMR context) but did not quote the text.
- Later amendments (2022-2025) to the regulations, and the FSSAI Ayurveda Aahara Regulations 2022 and Nutraceutical Regulations 2022 (their claim rules for "Ayurveda" foods and supplements), were not researched in this session. This is a known gap for supplement brands.
- The 2024 government push to drop "health drink" labelling (e.g. Bournvita) was not verified here.

## Q3. Drugs and Magic Remedies Act, CCPA misleading-ads and dark-patterns guidelines, Ayush rules

### Takeaway
Never let a script suggest a product treats, cures or prevents a listed disease or condition. Diabetes, obesity and sexual capacity are on the Drugs and Magic Remedies Act list, and breaking it is a criminal offence. The CCPA (consumer regulator) can fine brands and endorsers ₹10-50 lakh. Its 2022 rules say endorsements must be a genuine, current opinion, and disclaimers cannot contradict the main claim. Its 2023 dark-pattern rules ban false urgency ("only 2 left!") and similar tricks.

### Cited Findings
- Drugs and Magic Remedies (Objectionable Advertisements) Act 1954, Section 3, bans ads suggesting a drug for: procuring miscarriage or preventing conception; maintaining or improving "sexual pleasure" or capacity; menstrual disorders; and "the diagnosis, cure, mitigation, treatment or prevention" of any disease in the Schedule. — [India Code, Act text](https://www.indiacode.nic.in/indiacode/bitstream/123456789/1412/1/A1954-21.pdf); [Wikipedia summary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drugs_and_Magic_Remedies_(Objectionable_Advertisements)_Act,_1954)
- The Schedule has 54 conditions, including diabetes, obesity and sexual impotence. Penalty: up to 6 months' imprisonment for a first offence and up to 1 year for repeat offences. A 2024 draft amendment proposed a longer schedule, but whether it was enacted is not confirmed. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drugs_and_Magic_Remedies_(Objectionable_Advertisements)_Act,_1954); [draft amendment PDF](https://thetruthpill.in/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/draft-of-the-drugs-and-magic-remedies.pdf)
- Patanjali case: on 27 Feb 2024 the Supreme Court imposed an interim ban on Patanjali medicine ads claiming to treat Act-listed ailments. On 19 Mar 2024 it issued contempt notices to Ramdev and Balkrishna. — [WION](https://www.wionews.com/india-news/indias-top-court-slaps-ban-on-patanjali-medicine-ads-for-misleading-claims-694430/amp); [Newslaundry](https://www.newslaundry.com/2024/03/21/laws-archaic-but-regret-the-ads-patanjali-md-balakrishnan-apologise-to-sc-for-misleading-ads)
- Self-declaration: in 2024 the Supreme Court required advertisers to file a self-declaration that an ad complies with the law before it runs (TV and radio on the Broadcast Seva portal, print and digital on the Press Council portal). — [search summary of SC orders](https://www.medboundtimes.com/amp/story/medbound-blog/patanjalis-ad-controversy-regulatory-and-industry-impact). Exact order date (7 May 2024) and current status were not verified.
- Rule 170 (Drugs and Cosmetics Rules) required state pre-approval of Ayurvedic, Siddha and Unani drug ads. The Ayush Ministry omitted it on 1 July 2024. The Supreme Court stayed the omission on 27 Aug 2024, then vacated that stay on 11 Aug 2025 when it closed the IMA case. — [LiveLaw 2024](https://www.livelaw.in/amp/top-stories/supreme-court-ministry-of-ayush-drugs-and-cosmetics-rules-1945-omission-of-rule-170-stayed-267763); [LiveLaw 2025](https://www.livelaw.in/amp/top-stories/supreme-court-vacates-stay-on-ayush-ministrys-notification-that-permitted-advertisements-of-ayurvedic-unani-siddha-drugs-300536); [Bar & Bench](https://www.barandbench.com/amp/story/news/misleading-medical-ads-case-supreme-court-closes-case-allows-ayush-ads-without-state-approval)
- CCPA Guidelines for Prevention of Misleading Advertisements and Endorsements, 2022 (notified 9 June 2022) apply to all media, and to manufacturers, agencies and endorsers. — [Nishith Desai](https://www.nishithdesai.com/research-and-articles/hotline/technology-law-analysis/guidelines-on-misleading-advertisements-and-endorsements-notified-under-the-consumer-protection-act-2019-6172); [Vikaspedia](https://en.vikaspedia.in/social-welfare/social-awareness/consumer-education/guidelines-for-prevention-of-misleading-advertisements-and-endorsements-for-misleading-advertisements-2022)
- Key rules: ads must not exaggerate "scientific validity" or capability, or imply a claim is universally accepted when experts are divided. "Free" claims must state what the buyer must do. Disclaimers "should not contradict the material claim" and must be legible. Endorsements must reflect a "genuine, reasonably current opinion" based on adequate information or experience, and any material connection must be disclosed. For children's ads, health or nutrition claims must be scientifically substantiated by a recognised body, and junk food ads are barred in children's programming. — [Khaitan & Co](https://khaitanco.com/thought-leaderships/CCPA-releases-guidelines-for-Prevention-of-Misleading-Advertisements-and-Endorsements-for-Misleading-Advertisements-2022)
- Penalties under the Consumer Protection Act 2019: up to ₹10 lakh, up to ₹50 lakh for repeat offences. The CCPA can bar an endorser for up to 1 year, or 3 years for repeats. — [Khaitan & Co](https://khaitanco.com/thought-leaderships/CCPA-releases-guidelines-for-Prevention-of-Misleading-Advertisements-and-Endorsements-for-Misleading-Advertisements-2022); [Vikaspedia search summary](https://en.vikaspedia.in/social-welfare/social-awareness/consumer-education/guidelines-for-prevention-of-misleading-advertisements-and-endorsements-for-misleading-advertisements-2022)
- CCPA Dark Patterns Guidelines, notified 30 Nov 2023, list 13 practices, including false urgency (fake scarcity such as "Only 2 left! 30 others looking") and basket sneaking. They apply to platforms, advertisers and sellers. — [JSA](https://www.jsalaw.com/newsletters-and-updates/ccpa-issues-guidelines-for-prevention-and-regulation-of-dark-patterns-2023/); [Bar & Bench](https://www.barandbench.com/law-firms/view-point/ccpa-steps-in-to-curb-dark-patterns); [CCPA draft](https://consumeraffairs.nic.in/sites/default/files/file-uploads/latestnews/Draft%20Guidelines%20for%20Prevention%20and%20Regulation%20of%20Dark%20Patterns%202023.pdf)

### Inferences
- For tea, spices and ghee, words like "diabetes", "sugar control", "weight loss", "fat burner", "cures acidity" and "boosts stamina in bed" are the highest-risk words. They touch the Drugs and Magic Remedies Act Schedule and FSSAI's disease-claim ban at the same time.
- Countdown timers, "only today" or "last 3 packs" lines in a reel must be true. Otherwise they are false urgency under the 2023 guidelines.
- An ad's small-print disclaimer ("results may vary") cannot rescue a headline claim that is misleading.

### Gaps
- Current legal status of Rule 170 after the Aug 2025 order: sources suggest ads for Ayurvedic, Siddha and Unani drugs no longer need state pre-approval, but verify this.
- Whether the Supreme Court's self-declaration requirement still applies after the case closed in Aug 2025 was not confirmed.
- The final wording of all 13 dark patterns was not checked against the notified gazette text; some descriptions come from the draft.
- Ayush Ministry rules for Ayurvedic cosmetics and proprietary Ayurvedic medicine claims were not researched in depth.

## Q4. Meta Advertising Standards: health and wellness, personal attributes, before/after, weight loss, targeting

### Takeaway
On Meta, never say "you" plus a health or body condition ("Struggling with your belly fat?"). Do not show weight-loss before/after pictures or pinched fat. Do not make anyone feel bad about their body. Target weight-loss products and supplements at 18+. Since 2025, health-related brands also face limits on optimising for purchases, and audiences or conversions named after health conditions get disabled.

### Cited Findings
- Dietary, health and weight-loss or weight-gain products must target people aged 18 or over. Supplements are not exempt. General food (including protein products), non-permanent cosmetics and fitness services are exempt. — [Meta Health & Wellness standard](https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/restricted-goods-services/health-wellness) (page is JavaScript-rendered and could not be read directly; content via [Accelerated Digital Media 2026 guide](https://www.accelerateddigitalmedia.com/insights/guide-to-social-media-health-ad-restrictions-2026/))
- No targeting of under-18s with weight-loss products, cosmetic procedures, or "dietary, health or herbal supplements". — [Meta Cosmetic Procedures and Wellness](https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/content-specific-restrictions/cosmetic-procedures-and-wellness) (via ADM guide)
- Ads must not imply or generate "negative self-perception" or declare a perfect body type, to promote diet, weight loss, cosmetic or health products. Side-by-side before/after comparisons for weight loss are not allowed (fitness classes may be an exception). Close-ups of pinched fat and "statements of inferiority about physical appearance" are not allowed. — [Meta Health & Wellness](https://transparency.meta.com/en-gb/policies/ad-standards/restricted-goods-services/health-wellness) (via search snippets and the ADM guide)
- Conflict: the ADM guide says before/after is "permitted" for general cosmetic products and procedures targeted 18+, and does not describe a separate weight-loss rule. Search snippets of Meta's page say weight-loss before/after is not allowed. Treat weight-loss before/after as banned and cosmetic before/after as 18+ only. — [ADM](https://www.accelerateddigitalmedia.com/insights/guide-to-social-media-health-ad-restrictions-2026/) vs [Meta page snippet](https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/restricted-goods-services/health-wellness)
- Ads may not claim to "cure, heal, or eliminate" listed incurable diseases. Meta enforces against what the ad claims, not only what it shows. — [ADM 2026 guide](https://www.accelerateddigitalmedia.com/insights/guide-to-social-media-health-ad-restrictions-2026/)
- Personal attributes: ads may not "assert or imply" knowledge of a user's personal attributes, including medical conditions, physical or mental health, and financial status. In practice this means avoiding second-person copy like "Are you overweight?" or "conquer your depression". — [Meta Personal Attributes policy](https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/objectionable-content/privacy-violations-personal-attributes); [Meta personal health and appearance page](https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/objectionable-content/personal-health-and-appearance)
- Early 2025: Meta categorised health brands (those tied to medical conditions or health status). Most got a partial restriction on optimising for lower-funnel events such as purchases. Leading into 2026, custom audiences, lookalikes and custom conversions whose names imply sensitive traits (e.g. "arthritis_interest_list") were flagged and disabled. — [ADM 2026 guide](https://www.accelerateddigitalmedia.com/insights/guide-to-social-media-health-ad-restrictions-2026/); [PixelFlow docs](https://docs.pixelflow.so/troubleshooting/what-are-meta-s-health-and-wellness-restrictions-and-how-do-they-affect-tracking-ce3t3)

### Inferences
- A ghee or tea brand is "general food" and is probably not health-categorised, unless its claims or landing pages talk about conditions (diabetes, PCOS, weight loss). Health-condition copy can push a food brand into the restricted category and hurt purchase optimisation. This is an inference, not confirmed by Meta.
- A safe hook style is third-person or situational ("Morning chai that tastes like home") rather than diagnosis-style ("Is your gut weak?").

### Gaps
- The Meta Transparency Center pages could not be read directly in this session; the exact current wording of the Health & Wellness, Personal Attributes and "Sensational content" policies was not verified.
- Meta's "unrealistic outcomes" wording and its sensational-content rules (shocking or scary health imagery) were not confirmed from primary text.
- India-specific Meta rules, for example on Ayurvedic medicine ads, were not found.

## Q5. Practical do/don't wording for tea, spice and ghee scripts (built from the rules above)

### Takeaway
Safe scripts sell taste, ritual, origin, process and sensory facts the brand can prove. Risky scripts promise body or health outcomes, use "100%"/"pure"/"natural" loosely, or use fake testimonials or AI people. Each row below is an inference applying the cited rules, not a ruling.

### Cited Findings
These rows apply the sources cited in Q1-Q4.

| Category | Avoid in the script | Safer alternative (only if true and provable) | Rule behind it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tea | "Burns belly fat", "controls diabetes", "detox your liver" | "A light, refreshing cup after meals" | DMR Act Schedule (obesity, diabetes) — [India Code](https://www.indiacode.nic.in/indiacode/bitstream/123456789/1412/1/A1954-21.pdf); Meta weight-loss rules — [ADM](https://www.accelerateddigitalmedia.com/insights/guide-to-social-media-health-ad-restrictions-2026/) |
| Tea | "100% natural green tea" | "Just green tea leaves, nothing added" (single ingredient) | FSSAI "100%" advisory May 2025 — [Storyboard18](https://www.storyboard18.com/how-it-works/fssai-cracks-down-on-misleading-100-natural-claims-in-food-advertising-67782.htm); "natural" conditions — [Food Safety Works](https://foodsafetyworks.com/insights/conditions-behind-the-conditional-claims/) |
| Tea | "Boosts immunity, keeps you safe from pollution" | Describe ingredients and taste; health claims only if FSSAI-permitted and substantiated | ASCI chyawanprash decision — [ASCI CCC](https://www.ascionline.in/wp-content/uploads/2022/09/asci-april-18-ccc-recomm.pdf) |
| Spice | "Freshly ground masala" (packed) | "Ground in small batches", with the actual process | FSSAI "fresh" meaning — [Food Safety Works](https://foodsafetyworks.com/insights/conditions-behind-the-conditional-claims/) |
| Spice | "Haldi that cures joint pain / heals inflammation" | "The haldi our grandmothers cooked with" (heritage, not health) | FSSAI disease-claim ban (Gaps, Q2); DMR Act; ASCI Ayurveda decisions — [ASCI CCC](https://www.ascionline.in/wp-content/uploads/2022/09/asci-jan-18-ccc-recomm.pdf) |
| Spice | "Traditional recipe" (new blend) | Use only with 30-plus years' proof; otherwise "home-style recipe" | FSSAI "traditional" meaning — [Food Safety Works](https://foodsafetyworks.com/insights/conditions-behind-the-conditional-claims/) |
| Ghee | "100% pure desi ghee", "No. 1 ghee in India" | "Made only from cow's milk" (if true); "No. 1" only with independent data | FSSAI "100%" — [Aparajitha](https://www.aparajitha.com/fssai-directs-discontinuation-of-claims-on-food-labels-and-all-promotional-materials/); ASCI "No. 1" decisions — [exchange4media](https://www.exchange4media.com/advertising-news/asci-upholds-complaints-against-114-out-of-193-advertisements-86891.html) |
| Ghee | "Ayurvedic ghee for strong bones and sharp memory" | "Bilona-churned" (only if that is the process) | ASCI Ayurveda decisions; FSSAI health-claim rules |
| All | "Are you overweight?", "Tired of your acidity?" | "After a heavy meal, try..." (situational, third person) | Meta personal attributes — [Meta](https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/objectionable-content/privacy-violations-personal-attributes) |
| All | AI "customer" saying "I lost 5 kg with this tea" | AI presenter showing how to brew it, labelled "Video created using AI" | ASCI SGC guidelines (from about Dec 2026) — [PDF](https://foxmandal.in/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/Guidelines-for-Responsible-Labelling-of-Synthetically-Generated-Content-in-Advertising.pdf) |
| All | Creator reel without a label, or "#ad" buried in the caption | On-screen "Ad"/"Collaboration" for 3 s (≤15 s video) or one third of the length (15 s-2 min) | ASCI influencer rules — [PDF](https://www.ascionline.in/social/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/ASCI-Influencer-Guidelines.pdf) |
| All | Nutritionist-style "this prevents PCOS" from a non-qualified creator | A qualified dietician with credentials shown upfront, or only generic taste and use content | ASCI Addendum 2 (Aug 2023) — [PDF](https://www.ascionline.in/social/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/ASCI-Influencer-Guidelines.pdf) |
| All | "Only 3 packs left, offer ends tonight" (untrue) | Real stock and real deadline only | CCPA dark patterns 2023 — [JSA](https://www.jsalaw.com/newsletters-and-updates/ccpa-issues-guidelines-for-prevention-and-regulation-of-dark-patterns-2023/) |
| All | "Free gift" with a hidden condition | State the condition in the script and on screen | CCPA 2022 "free" rules — [Khaitan](https://khaitanco.com/thought-leaderships/CCPA-releases-guidelines-for-Prevention-of-Misleading-Advertisements-and-Endorsements-for-Misleading-Advertisements-2022) |

### Inferences
- A simple gate for scripts: every claim line needs (a) the exact label or lab proof that backs it, (b) no disease or body-outcome words, (c) none of "100%", "pure", "natural", "fresh", "traditional", "original" unless the FSSAI meaning fits, (d) no "you" plus a condition, and (e) the AI or creator labels above. This fits DTJ rule 5 (never fabricate claims or reviews).
- Skincare (cosmetics) sits under the Drugs and Cosmetics rules and ASCI, not FSSAI. "Removes acne permanently" or "whitening" style claims are high risk. Not researched in depth here.

### Gaps
- No primary-source list of FSSAI-permitted health claims for tea polyphenols, curcumin or ghee was found. Any functional claim needs a regulatory check first.
- Cosmetic-claim rules (CDSCO Cosmetics Rules 2020, ASCI guidance on fairness and skin claims) were not researched in this session.
- Not verified: whether Meta treats Ayurvedic or "herbal" food products as supplements, which would make 18+ targeting compulsory.
