# Indian D2C short-form video ad scripts (Reels, Meta, Shorts), 2023-2026

Research notes, 22 Oct 2026. 17 tool calls (web search + fetch). Several sources were blocked (exchange4media returned 403) or were vendor blogs; reliability is marked on each line. Many named-brand questions (Blue Tea 3D characters, Mamaearth, boAt, Sleepy Owl, Minimalist, Kapiva, Plix, Sugar) returned NO usable sourced material in this pass. See the Gaps sections; do not fill them from memory.

## 1. What works for Indian audiences: trust signals, price/value, COD, festive moments, humour

### Takeaway
The best sourced Indian data says trust comes from reviews, creators and checking the brand, not from celebrities. YouTube reviews (37% in Tier 3), creators (23%) and word of mouth (22%) matter; celebrity endorsement influences only about 3%. In Meta's festive study, two-thirds trust brands that work with credible creators. Festive ads should lead with concrete deals (percent off, bundles, bank/EMI offers). The festive window runs July-August to New Year, not just Diwali. Shark Tank India gives trust, but the producer has had ads using show clips taken down, so "as seen on Shark Tank" footage is a legal risk. Topical, filmy humour works when it is tied to one product insight and avoids politics, religion and personal attacks (Wakefit).

### Cited Findings
**Trust sources (Tier 2/3)**
- Rukam Capital with YouGov, 5,000+ respondents across 18 states, published 16 Jan 2026:
  - 37% of Tier 3 consumers rely on YouTube reviews at the start of the purchase journey.
  - 32% discover new products on social media; 23% trust creator recommendations; celebrity endorsements influence about 3%.
  - Word of mouth influences 22% (Tier 2 and 3), and 43% of Tier 3 consumers check the brand's official website before buying.
  - UPI is the main payment mode for 67%; 52% of Tier 2 consumers track sale days.
  - Source: [Storyboard18](https://www.storyboard18.com/brand-marketing/37-of-tier-3-consumers-rely-on-youtube-reviews-celebrity-influence-drops-to-3-report-87756.htm)
- Tier 2 women (Chrome Brand Pulse; date not confirmed): over 75% favour authenticity and verified reviews over influencer endorsements, and 71% have abandoned carts over price or quality worries. — [afaqs](https://www.afaqs.com/companies/tier-2-women-emerge-as-indias-new-e-commerce-trendsetters-10537407)
- Capterra India survey of 500 consumers (date unclear): 88% say the number of negative reviews matters. Marketplaces (44%) and review sites (43%) are the most-used review sources. — [Capterra India](https://www.capterra.in/blog/6479/online-shopping-trends-and-landscape)
- Practitioner view (opinion, not data): COD works as a "trust bridge" for a first order from an unknown brand, and reviews with photos are a strong trust lever. — [eChai Ventures](https://echai.ventures/d2c/d2c-cro-store-design/trust-signals-for-new-unknown-indian-brand)
- OLDER (mid-2010s Google India survey of 6,859 people): 67% of buyers complained that returns were complex, and 55% of non-buyers did not trust online product quality. — cited via search summary of [WARC](https://www.warc.com/newsandopinion/news/indias-online-shoppers-to-treble/en-gb/33918). Not verified first-hand.

**Creators and festive season (Meta's own data)**
- Source for all bullets below: [Meta Newsroom, 24 Sep 2025](https://about.fb.com/news/2025/09/genai-creators-and-reels-are-reshaping-festive-shopping-in-india/). Meta's "2025 Meta Digital Utsav" Festive Playbook draws on an IPSOS study Meta commissioned; the figures are self-reported by Meta.
- Creators:
  - Nearly half of festive shoppers follow creators.
  - Two-thirds say brands that work with credible creators earn their trust.
  - Over 30% rely on creator reviews and buying guides; nearly 1 in 2 find creator content most helpful for deals.
- Reels is the most influential format and the top choice for discovering trends, offers and gift ideas.
- The festive season runs from July-August through New Year's Eve, with "multiple opportunities... beyond Diwali".
- Meta tells advertisers to highlight:
  - percent-off sales, exclusive bundles, flash deals, and bank or EMI offers;
  - authentic, story-led content on craftsmanship, values or festive feeling;
  - festive colours and seasonal sound design.
- Shopper segments:
  - Gen Z (18-24) is more price-sensitive and wants discounts and promo codes.
  - In Tier 2/3, 63% used local e-commerce platforms but still favour in-person shopping and physical payments, which supports a COD message.
  - 44% of mothers made festive purchases on Meta platforms.
- 78% of festive shopping conversations happen on Meta platforms, especially WhatsApp.
- Trade coverage says Meta added language and region filters to Instagram's creator marketplace for India's varied festive moments. — [exchange4media](https://www.exchange4media.com/festive-season-news/meta-bets-on-ai-and-reels-to-power-festive-sales-in-india-147214.html), [Campaign India](https://www.campaignindia.in/article/meta-rolls-out-festive-tools-for-indias-ad-market/504618)

**Shark Tank India**
- YourStory, May 2024: Sony Pictures Networks India sent legal notices and had ads using show clips taken down.
  - Mintree's founder had to recreate the brand's ads.
  - Allter's founder had to stop all ads within 15 days of the season ending. She said sales did not change much, but trade and distributor trust rose.
  - Source: [YourStory](https://ts.yourstory.com/2024/05/beyond-shark-tank-india-mintree-allter-quirksmith-turms-d2c-brands)
- 2023: Hammer's co-founder reported about INR 30 lakh revenue in one day after the episode. A founder said the show lets you sell without people treating it as an ad and skipping it. — [Social Samosa, Mar 2023](https://www.socialsamosa.com/2023/03/shark-tank-india-the-marketing-platform)
- Blue Tea India appeared on Shark Tank India and got INR 75 lakh from Aman Gupta (boAt). This comes from a search snippet; the page was not opened. — [Inc42](https://inc42.com/?p=506714)

**Desi humour and filmy parody (Wakefit)**
- Source for all bullets below: [afaqs, 27 Dec 2024](https://www.afaqs.com/marketing/how-wakefit-spins-zany-internet-antics-into-mattress-marketing-gold-8568186)
- Wakefit's "Gaddagiri" is four short films named after the 1992 Bollywood film *Dadagiri*.
  - Hinglish tagline: "Lafda tab hota, jab tum barabar nai sota".
  - Each film rides a viral news moment (for example the 70-hour work week debate, and Poonam Pandey's fake-death stunt with her taking part).
  - One insight ties them together: bad sleep explains bad behaviour.
- CMO Kunal Dubey's rules:
  - No political, religious or personal humour.
  - If a real person is referenced, bring them in directly.
  - Test with neutral groups.
  - About 30 ideas are rejected for every 3 released.
  - The second ad was made in 12 hours.
  - Results: over 1 million organic interactions. Dubey says media works "three times better" when the story or idea is strong. Spend is about 90% digital.
- Counter-example: Bombay Shaving Company's newspaper ad about a UP board topper (Prachi Nigam) drew wide criticism for being insensitive. ASCI responded, and Caresmith parodied the ad. — [afaqs](https://www.afaqs.com/news/advertising/caresmith-india-takes-a-jab-at-bombay-shaving-companys-controversial-ad), [Storyboard18](https://www.storyboard18.com/brand-marketing/head-to-head-caresmith-takes-a-jab-at-bombay-shaving-companys-viral-campaign-30756.htm)

### Inferences
- Use "real people" proof first: review screenshots, creator demos, customer counts the brand can prove. Celebrity-style framing earns little trust (3%).
- For first-time buyers in Tier 2/3, a spoken "COD available" plus an easy-returns line fits the data. Physical payments are still favoured and returns complexity is a known fear. The direct COD-in-ad evidence is practitioner opinion only.
- Festive scripts should name a concrete offer (bundle, percent off, gift pack) and a moment. There are moments from Raksha Bandhan (August) through New Year, not only Diwali.
- Do not use Shark Tank India clips or logos in paid ads. A spoken founder line ("we pitched on Shark Tank India") is lower risk but still unverified legally.
- Humour: one product insight per joke, topical but not political, religious or about a person (the BSC backlash shows the cost of punching at a real private person).

### Gaps
- No sourced data on which specific trust claims convert better in Indian ads: "X lakh customers", doctor or expert, made in India, Ayurveda or heritage, mother or family approval.
- No Indian A/B data on COD or returns messages inside ad creative.
- No sourced performance data on monsoon or wedding-season creative.
- No primary data on Shark Tank-mention ads after 2024.

## 2. Language: Hindi vs Hinglish vs English vs regional (Gujarati), natural spoken copy, caption script

### Takeaway
Creator supply and engagement are heavily Hindi and regional. Kofluence 2025 says 68% of creators work mainly in Hindi and 24% in regional languages. Tier 3/4 creators get higher engagement (4.5-5.5% vs 3-4% in metros). Hinglish is the default bridge between metro and small-town audiences. For Gujarat, an older practitioner piece recommends mostly-Gujarati copy for 45+ women and household FMCG, and English-led copy for urban metro pilots. Hard Meta or Google India data on vernacular ad conversion was not found; most uplift figures are vendor or trade claims.

### Cited Findings
- Kofluence "Decoding Influence: 2025 Influencer Marketing Report" (~1,000 creators, 50 brand managers; self-reported):
  - 68.2% of creators make content mainly in Hindi; 23.9% in regional languages.
  - Over 62% got more vernacular-language briefs from brands.
  - Tier 3/4 creators run 43-48% of campaigns, with 4.5-5.5% engagement vs 3-4% in metros.
  - 84.5% say short-form video is their best-paying category.
  - Only 17.2% of brands have a separate regional budget.
  - Source: [NetInfluencer](https://www.netinfluencer.com/india-influencer-marketing-industry-estimated-at-400m-as-regional-creators-reshape-brand-strategy/), [Kofluence](https://www.kofluence.com/2025-influencer-marketing-report-sneak-peek/), [Storyboard18](https://www.storyboard18.com/amp/how-it-works/creator-economy-in-india-hits-rs-3500-crore-fueled-by-ecomm-fmcg-sectors-kofluence-73457.htm)
- MICA professor Falguni Vasadeva: Hinglish is used to reach both Hindi and English speakers, in metros and in smaller towns or rural areas. Brands ask her for Hinglish because it reaches a wider audience. This is qualitative. — [MICA repository](https://ir.mica.ac.in/entities/publication/17b86fb2-f287-431a-82cb-8fd7abadb2d2/full)
- Trade claim (exchange4media, as summarised by search; the page returned 403 on fetch):
  - Nearly 70% of users prefer digital content in their local language.
  - Vernacular campaigns get 2x the engagement of English- or Hindi-only ads (no primary source given).
  - Rapido's marketing head reported 20-30% more ROI from vernacular content, especially in the South, Maharashtra, Gujarat and Kolkata.
  - Source: [exchange4media](https://www.exchange4media.com/advertising-news/from-challenge-to-opportunity-vernacular-advertising-sees-2x-growth-in-engagement-rates-141481.html)
- Google-KPMG (older, figure via secondary source): 70% of Indians find vernacular digital content more reliable than English. — [Storyboard18](https://stgcms.storyboard18.com/?p=710)
- OLDER (2011, Ozone Media): regional-language ads got up to 30% more clicks than English ads. — [exchange4media](https://www.exchange4media.com/digital-news/regional-language-ads-outperform-english-ads-online-41517.html)
- Meta India (2023, via search summary): a little under half of content would be non-Hindi, non-English regional-language content. — [Outlook Business, Sandhya Devanathan](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/magazine/sandhya-devanathan-writes-rise-of-regional-creators-will-drive-next-gen-creativity)
- Meta added regional fonts to its Edits app and India-specific Reels tools. — [Storyboard18](https://www.storyboard18.com/amp/digital/meta-rolls-out-india-specific-instagram-reels-tools-and-new-regional-fonts-for-edits-app-84992.htm)
- Gujarat (OLDER, Social Samosa, Jan 2020):
  - For FMCG aimed at women 45+, speaking Gujarati helps. For a Gujarat-only spice brand, it suggests about 80% Gujarati and 10-15% Hindi.
  - An Ahmedabad-only urban startup pilot used English-heavy copy with some Hindi.
  - Source: [Social Samosa](https://www.socialsamosa.com/2020/01/state-of-social-media-marketing-in-gujarat)
- Suhana Masala made a Gujarat-specific spice range TVC around a Gujarati household character (date unconfirmed). — [Storyboard18](https://www.storyboard18.com/quantum-brief/suhana-masala-launches-new-ad-campaign-promotes-new-gujarat-spice-range-15297.htm)
- Vendor advice (Koro, an AI video tool; unverified):
  - Use 9:16 with Romanised Hinglish subtitles for sound-off viewers, and a Hook, Problem, Solution, Proof, CTA structure.
  - It also claims Hindi or Hinglish UGC beats English in Tier 2/3, with no data.
  - Source: [Koro blog](https://getkoro.app/blog/how-to-make-ai-ugc-video-in-hindi-2026-step-by-step-india)
- Vendor or media-desk claim (IRPR): Gujarati creative should be localised in tone, references and on-screen text, not just translated. — [IRPR](https://reach.irpr.agency/locations/gujarat/tv9-gujarati/)

### Inferences
- Default: spoken Hinglish voiceover with Romanised Hinglish captions for pan-India Reels. Use Gujarati voiceover for Gujarat-targeted household, food or FMCG ads aimed at older women or family buyers. Keep English for premium metro audiences. Test by language rather than assume.
- Natural spoken copy means everyday code-mixing: English nouns for products and benefits ("skin", "protein", "order") inside Hindi or Gujarati sentence structure, as in Wakefit's "barabar nai sota" (colloquial spelling, not textbook Hindi). Avoid "shuddh" or Sanskritised words a person would not say aloud.
- Caption script: no source compares Devanagari or Gujarati script against Roman on Reels. Roman script is the vendor default. Native script may suit 45+ Gujarati audiences, but this is untested.

### Gaps
- No Meta or Google India report with ad conversion (CVR, ROAS) by language was found.
- No sourced data on Gujarati Reels ad performance; no data on Marathi or Tamil.
- No study of caption script (Roman vs Devanagari vs Gujarati) on retention.

## 3. Named brand examples and founder or creative-head interviews

### Takeaway
Sourced detail exists for four brands:
- **The Whole Truth:** ingredient-honesty claims ("Pure Protein. No Fillers."), mostly bottom-funnel static ads with discount lines.
- **Two Brothers Organic Farms:** founder and farmer storytelling ("Know Your Farmer", farm videos, real farmer faces, education-first).
- **Wakefit:** filmy, topical Hinglish humour.
- **Blue Tea:** Shark Tank backing.

For the other named brands, nothing verifiable turned up in this pass.

### Cited Findings
- The Whole Truth (Hawky analysis, 18 Dec 2025, 198 ads, May-Dec 2025):
  - 158 Meta and 40 Google ads; 81% bottom-funnel; about 64% promote whey protein.
  - Themes: Clean 18%, Discounts 16%, Transparency 13%, Healthcare 12%, Purity 10%.
  - Formats: 68% static, 18% video.
  - Copy: "Order now" in 79% of ads; lines like "Cleanest, Lightest Whey Protein" and "Pure Protein. No Fillers."; rupee discount hooks ("Save up to ₹850").
  - The analysis did not show founder-led or label-reading video as a measured tactic.
  - Source: [Hawky](https://hawky.ai/blog/decoding-the-whole-truth)
- The Whole Truth ran a 2026 campaign with a reality-TV personality (Parul Gulati) and a no-filter tone (details thin). — [Fueler](https://fueler.io/amangarg/parul-gulati-the-traitors-reunion-the-whole-truth-aman-garg)
- Two Brothers Organic Farms:
  - Campaigns "Know Your Farmer" and "Food Heals" educate rather than sell.
  - Satyajit Hange says storytelling across Instagram, blogs, farm videos and farm visits is grounded in "real people and real processes", showing farmers' real faces.
  - Source: [Indian Retailer](https://indianretailer.com/interview/retail-people/new-commerce/two-brothers-organic-farms-targets-100-percent-growth-eyes)
- Two Brothers' brand film (Independence Day week) tells the founders' story of leaving corporate jobs for their village (Bhodani, Maharashtra). It shows the kitchens and traceability tech. — [MediaNews4U](https://www.medianews4u.com/two-brothers-organic-farms-unveils-brand-film-calling-for-a-fix-to-indias-broken-food-system/)
- Zerodha's write-up says Two Brothers' marketing focuses on education: people who understand industrial food will pay more for organic. — [Zerodha Z-Connect](https://zerodha.com/z-connect/wp-json/wp/v2/posts/404638)
- Wakefit: see section 1 ([afaqs](https://www.afaqs.com/marketing/how-wakefit-spins-zany-internet-antics-into-mattress-marketing-gold-8568186)).
- Bombay Shaving Company: L&K Saatchi & Saatchi won its creative mandate ([afaqs](https://afaqs.com/news/advertising/lk-saatchi-saatchi-bags-the-creative-mandate-for-bombay-shaving-company)). It also faced the topper-ad backlash (section 1).
- Blue Tea India: Kolkata company founded 2018 by Nitesh Singh and Sunil Chandra Saha; best sellers include butterfly pea, chamomile, "belly fat" and hibiscus teas. — [YourStory 2021, OLDER](https://yourstory.com/2021/09/kolkata-flower-tea-startup-blue-tea-india-aiming-global-market), [Inc42](https://inc42.com/company/blue-tea-india/)
- A Blue Tea Instagram post mentions an "AI toy makeover" of its wellness range (via an aggregator, not verified). — [Particl](https://www.particl.com/company/bluetea)

### Inferences
- Ingredient-honesty brands script around one checkable claim ("no fillers", "no added sugar") plus a rupee offer at the bottom of the funnel. The Whole Truth's paid ads are more offer-heavy than its brand image suggests.
- Founder or farmer brands (Two Brothers) win on showing the real place and people. The script structure is origin, then process, then why it costs more.

### Gaps
- **Blue Tea:** 3D ingredient-character ads, founder-led videos and the 14-day timeline were NOT found in any source. Check Blue Tea's Meta Ad Library and Instagram directly.
- **Mamaearth, boAt, Sleepy Owl, Minimalist, Kapiva, Plix, Sugar Cosmetics:** no sourced ad-creative strategy or creative-head interview found (searches returned generic results).
- **Two Brothers:** no sourced detail on A2 ghee or khapli atta ad scripts.

## 4. Indian creator / UGC ecosystem: how creator partnership ads are scripted

### Takeaway
Short-form creator video is the main paid format. Brands increasingly brief in vernacular languages and use small-town creators for higher engagement. Credible-creator content builds trust (Meta: two-thirds), and Tier 3 shoppers lean on YouTube reviews. No agency-published script guidance (Winkl, Qoruz, Good Creator Co, The Moms Co cases) was found.

### Cited Findings
- 84.5% of creators say short-form video is their best-paying category.
  - Instagram gets over 50% of influencer spend.
  - E-commerce (23%) and FMCG (19%) are the top spending sectors.
  - Over 62% of creators get more vernacular briefs.
  - Source: [NetInfluencer, Kofluence 2025](https://www.netinfluencer.com/india-influencer-marketing-industry-estimated-at-400m-as-regional-creators-reshape-brand-strategy/)
- India's influencer market is about Rs 3,000-3,500 crore in 2025, growing about 22% a year. — [Storyboard18](https://www.storyboard18.com/amp/how-it-works/creator-economy-in-india-hits-rs-3500-crore-fueled-by-ecomm-fmcg-sectors-kofluence-73457.htm)
- Over 30% of festive shoppers rely on creator reviews and buying guides, and two-thirds trust brands that work with credible creators. — [Meta Newsroom](https://about.fb.com/news/2025/09/genai-creators-and-reels-are-reshaping-festive-shopping-in-india/)
- 37% of Tier 3 consumers start with YouTube reviews; 23% trust creator recommendations. — [Storyboard18 / Rukam Capital](https://www.storyboard18.com/brand-marketing/37-of-tier-3-consumers-rely-on-youtube-reviews-celebrity-influence-drops-to-3-report-87756.htm)
- Indian Meta creative-strategist job specs ask for direct-response hooks, emotional triggers and cultural cues, with Hinglish storytelling a plus (weak source: job listing). — [Marketing Monk job board](https://www.marketingmonk.so/jobboard/jobs/creative-strategist-manager-at-intentfarm-9zo2ti)

### Inferences
- Creator ads aimed at Tier 2/3 should look like a review: the creator uses the product, shows the result, and says the price or offer. Hindi or regional-language creators fit where supply and engagement are highest.

### Gaps
- No Winkl, Qoruz, Good Creator Co or The Moms Co script guidance or case studies found.
- No sourced creator-brief templates from Indian agencies.
