# Short-form UGC ad scripts: hooks (first 1-3 s), retention, and writing for the ear (TikTok, Reels, Meta), 2024-2026

Research notes, 22-23 Oct 2026. Every finding has a source link. Many benchmark numbers come from secondary roundups (vendors, agencies) and are labelled as such. Older sources (pre-2024) are marked [OLDER].

---

## 1. What hook types do pros use, with example lines?

### Takeaway
Practitioners agree that a hook is a stack of three layers fired together in the first 1-3 seconds: a spoken line, a visual, and on-screen text. Motion's framework names about ten tactic families (contrarian, call-out, question, pain, social proof, curiosity gap, authority, story, urgency, transformation). Savannah Sanchez and Billo add format-level "visual hooks" (skits, reveals, handwriting, text-message styling). The clearest test: at least one of four conditions must fire in the first 1-3 s (an open loop, something that feels about the viewer, a pattern interrupt, or a clear promise of what the next ~30 s gives).

### Cited Findings

**Hook = three layers (hook stacking at second 0)**
- Motion's Hook Analysis framework splits a video hook into the spoken hook ("first words or first VO line"), the visual hook (what is on screen in the first 1-3 s) and the text-overlay hook (first headline the eye lands on). It rates whether hook and visual are "Aligned / Complementary / Misaligned". Last updated 17 Apr 2026. — [Motion, Hook Analysis framework](https://motionapp.com/library/frameworks/hook-analysis)
- Motion's "1-3 second test": at least one of four must fire: open loop, self-relevant response, pattern interrupt, or a clear promise about the next 30 seconds. — [Motion, Hook Analysis](https://motionapp.com/library/frameworks/hook-analysis)
- Motion's "clarity vs curiosity" rule: too much clarity removes the reason to keep watching; too much curiosity reads as clickbait and costs trust. Aim for enough clarity to feel relevant and enough curiosity to feel unresolved. — [Motion, Hook Analysis](https://motionapp.com/library/frameworks/hook-analysis)
- Motion's named tactics: Contrarian, Demographic Callout, Question, Pain Agitation, Social Proof, Curiosity Gap, Authority, Storytelling, Urgency, Transformation. Eight triggers: Pattern Interrupt, Pain Agitation, Curiosity Gap, Identity Call-Out, Social Proof/Credibility, Aspiration/Desire, Urgency/Stakes, Contrarian/Myth-Busting. Motion also says a hook should match the viewer's awareness stage (Unaware to Most-Aware). — [Motion, Hook Analysis](https://motionapp.com/library/frameworks/hook-analysis)
- Sanchez: pair an interesting written hook with an editing effect where possible. Don't let a creator go straight into a testimonial with no hook first. — [Motion talk, Savannah Sanchez "15 Proven UGC Ad Hooks", Jul 2026, 11:50 and 13:25](https://motionapp.com/library/talk/15-proven-ugc-ad-hooks-that-are-working-right-now-savannah-sanchez/)
- "Hook stacking" across the whole ad (not only second 0): Hook -> hook tied to the problem/solution -> hook tied to value props -> hook tied to social proof -> hook tied to the CTA. Give the viewer a new reason to keep watching "every 5-15 seconds". [OLDER, Apr 2023] — [UGC Social newsletter, "Hook stacking"](https://ugcsocial.substack.com/p/hook-stacking-the-secret-to-longer)

**Named hook formats with template lines (Savannah Sanchez, Jul 2026)** — [Motion talk](https://motionapp.com/library/talk/15-proven-ugc-ad-hooks-that-are-working-right-now-savannah-sanchez/)
- Running "Yapper" (talking to camera while moving): "3 Reasons Why I Think Everyone Should Start ___." She calls yapper ads "rebranded UGC": more talking to camera, less B-roll, should feel like a friend's recommendation.
- Surprise reveal: "OMG what is that... (grabs product) it's a ___!!"
- Self-skit (one creator plays two roles): "POV: The girl/guy that just found out about ___."
- Mission find (magnifying-glass edit): "Mission: Find the perfect ___, complete!"
- Moderation twist: "All things in life are better in moderation... except ___."
- Text-exchange / chat styling: "___ entered the chat."
- Story time: "As I've gotten older, I've realized..."
- Delivery: "I exercised my use of free will a few days ago..." then doorbell and package.
- Car chat: talking in a car, sometimes cut with B-roll; she says car-set ads perform very well.
- Visual-only hooks: spin the bottle, quick outfit change (clap/swipe transition), swipe through product options, big bold or handwritten text.
- She says evergreen trends beat moment-specific ones.

**More hook families (Billo, 24 Sep 2026)** — [Billo, "Best Performing UGC Hooks"](https://billo.app/blog/ugc-hooks/)
- Comment reply: "So I got this question..." or a text overlay quoting a real viewer comment, or answering a common myth.
- Honest truth / confessional: "This is sponsored, but here's what actually happened..." Billo says in its H1 2026 health-ad data, confessional hooks converted even without a standout hook rate.
- Pattern interrupt: creator drops something then snaps into frame; hard cut between unrelated scenes; bold sound effect with a visual shift; flash or jump zoom.
- Unique angle: camera peeking out of a bag; reveal as a fridge door swings open; product found in a glove box.
- Handwriting: creator writes the message live on a mirror, notebook or cake instead of a graphic overlay.
- Funny skit/dialogue: roommate drama, couple fighting over the last gummy; the punchline should land in the first few seconds.
- Voiceless: no spoken line; eye contact, gestures, captions, product focus; suits sound-off viewing.
- Mix-and-match: e.g. honest truth + comment reply.

**Openers from Motion's UGC guide** — [Motion/Runneth, "How to create effective UGC ads"](https://runneth.com/blog/how-to-create-effective-ugc-ads) (seen via search summary; page redirected from motionapp.com and was not fetched in full)
- Problem-solution: "I used to struggle with..."; transformation: "Here's what happened when I tried..."; social proof: "Everyone's been asking about..."

**Hook testing practice**
- Sanchez: "The most important variable that you can change in your ad is the hook itself" (Jun 2024). "I really think the hook is the most important part of the ad in that first three seconds" (Jan 2026). Iterate by swapping only the first few seconds and keeping the body constant. Changing only the text on top is not seen by Facebook as a different variation (Jan 2026, 28:17). — [Motion expert page: Savannah Sanchez](https://motionapp.com/library/expert/savannah-sanchez/)
- Film 3-5 different hooks per ad (attributed to Dara Denney's teaching, secondhand). — [UGC Ads database, issue 97](https://ugcads.beehiiv.com/p/ugc-ad-examples-97)
- Write 3-5 hook variations per brief: lead with a problem, a result, a question, and a controversial opinion. — [Cometly, UGC ads](https://www.cometly.com/post/ugc-ads)

**Alex Hormozi (primary text not retrieved)**
- Third-party "Hormozi-style" frameworks describe a hook as a call-out (name who it is for) plus a tension type (curiosity, fear, desire, contrarian, proof), and stress specific numbers and names over vague claims. These are community derivatives, not the book. — [SkillsMP "Hormozi Hook Writer"](https://skillsmp.com/creators/karausab590-ops/clawads-marketing-skills/alex-hormozi-hook-writer)
- Secondhand story: Hormozi cut ~3 s of build-up from a video's opening and views rose from about 40,000 to 780,000. Unverified against his channel. — [Skool post](https://www.skool.com/lead-gen-mastery-4994/why-your-content-fails-in-the-first-3-seconds-and-how-to-fix-it)
- Secondhand: "Promise -> Proof -> Plan" as his sequence. — [Skool post](https://www.skool.com/lead-gen-mastery-4994/why-your-content-fails-in-the-first-3-seconds-and-how-to-fix-it)

### Inferences
- A practical hook spec for a scriptwriter: write the three layers as one unit. Example: VO "I almost returned this." + visual: product half back in its box + text: "Day 3 vs Day 30". Make sure at least one of Motion's four conditions fires, and the hook matches the awareness stage of the audience being targeted.
- "Call-out" hooks (naming the viewer, e.g. "If you run a store and your ads get clicks but no orders...") are the safest kind for cold traffic, because Barry Hott (section 2) and Motion both put relevance ahead of raw attention.

### Gaps
- I did not retrieve Hormozi's own $100M Leads hook chapter or a transcript. Commonly repeated lists (labels, yes-questions, if-then, ridiculous results; or headline elements like recency, relevance, celebrity, proximity, conflict, unusual, ongoing) could not be confirmed from a primary source here. The second list matches classic journalism "news values", which is what search returned. Verify in the book before quoting.
- No Foreplay, Insense or Jake Abrahams primary article on hook types was found in this pass.
- One secondary source claims Meta bans graphic overlays, logos or watermarks in a video ad's first 3 seconds ([UGC Ads database](https://ugcads.beehiiv.com/p/ugc-ad-examples-97), via search summary). I found no Meta policy page that confirms this. Treat it as unverified and do not use it as a rule.

## 2. What do named experts say about the first 1-3 seconds, and what are the measured benchmarks?

### Takeaway
On Meta, "hook rate" or "thumbstop" means 3-second video plays divided by impressions. Motion's own bands: below 25% needs work, 25-35% solid, above 35% strong. Secondary roundups citing Motion's 2026 dataset put median cold in-feed DTC at about 23%, cold Reels about 30%, warm retargeting about 36%. TikTok's own research: over 63% of its highest-CTR auction ads show the key message or product in the first 3 seconds. Experts agree the hook is the main lever. Two strong caveats: Barry Hott says relevance beats hook rate (he has seen winners below 15%), and Dara Denney says the creator matters more than the script or hook.

### Cited Findings

**Definitions and benchmarks**
- Motion defines thumbstop rate (also called hook rate) as 3-second video plays / impressions x 100. Bands: <25% needs work, 25-35% solid, >35% strong. — [Motion blog, key creative performance metrics](https://motionapp.com/blog/key-creative-performance-metrics) (via search summary; URL now redirects to runneth.com)
- Motion's metrics cheat sheet: thumbstop ratio = "% of people watching the first 2-3 seconds of your video (varies by platform)". TikTok hold rate = "% of users who watched the first 6s, or completely played". Meta hold equivalent = 15s/3s retention. One hard threshold: if Meta first-frame retention is under 90%, change the first frame. Otherwise compare to your own account average. — [Motion Help, Metrics cheat sheet](https://help.motionapp.com/en/articles/7730931-metrics-cheat-sheet)
- Secondary 2026 medians for hook rate (3s views / impressions): Feed cold DTC prospecting 18-28% (~23%); Reels cold DTC 24-36% (~30%); Stories 22-32% (~27%); retargeting 30-45% (~36%); kill signal for cold traffic below 15%. Sources: Sepia Lab (citing Motion's 2026 in-feed dataset), adlibrary, MHI, Affect Group. AdSights calls these ranges "directional" and notes Meta publishes no official hook-rate benchmark. — [AdSights, 2026 video creative benchmarks](https://www.adsights.ai/resources/guides/video-creative-metrics-benchmarks-2026-guide)
- Motion's 2026 Creative Benchmarks study is described secondhand as 578,750 creatives, 6,015 advertiser accounts and $1.29B spend. — via search summary of [Sepia Lab](https://sepia-lab.com/en/blog/hook-rate-benchmarks) / [Taylor Sicard](https://taylorsicard.com/blog/meta-ads-creative-benchmarks-2026); primary report not opened.
- Billo cites an average Meta hook rate of about 24%. — via [AdSights](https://www.adsights.ai/resources/guides/video-creative-metrics-benchmarks-2026-guide)
- TikTok benchmarks conflict across vendors: 30-35% baseline with 40%+ elite in one guide; 20-35% average and 50%+ excellent in another. Some divide by plays, not impressions. — search summary of [Segwise glossary](https://segwise.ai/glossary/hook-rate), [Nestscale](https://nestscale.com/?p=47200), [PostEverywhere](https://posteverywhere.ai/social-media-terms/hook-rate)
- TikTok (official): "over 63% of all videos with the highest CTR highlight their key message or product within the first 3 seconds". Also: vertical 9:16 videos average 25% higher 6-second watch-through; 1 in 3 highest-VTR auction ads "break the fourth wall" (creator talks to camera); 40% of highest-VTR auction ads use text overlays; tracks above 120 BPM often lift VTR. [OLDER, 21 Oct 2020, but still TikTok's own published guidance] — [TikTok for Business, 9 creative tips](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/en-US/blog/9-creative-tips-to-drive-auction-ad-performance)
- About 90% of ad recall happens in the first 6 seconds (Billo citing Sprout Social; primary not checked). — [Billo](https://billo.app/blog/ugc-hooks/)

**Expert positions**
- Barry Hott (via business partner Alex Cooper, 10 Apr 2025): "Nobody cares what you have to say unless it relates to them clearly and instantly." "Pretty ads stand out and get skipped." "Marketers love clever ads. Consumers ignore them." Prioritise hook relevance over hook rate; Cooper notes winning ads with hook rates under 15%. — [Alex Cooper, 7 lessons from Barry Hott](https://alexcooper.beehiiv.com/p/7-facebook-advertising-lessons-i-learned-from-barry-hott)
- Hott: copy ideas from the organic feed (camera angles, lighting, audio, pacing), not from competitors' ads. — [same](https://alexcooper.beehiiv.com/p/7-facebook-advertising-lessons-i-learned-from-barry-hott); Hott also recommends using customers' own language (undated webinar recap) — [Northbeam](https://northbeam.io/post/unleashing-the-power-of-ugly-ads)
- Dara Denney: "Our brains crave resolution. Users will stay longer to complete the loop." She studies YouTubers for curiosity because YouTube is opt-in (Oct 2025). "Your creator and the talent that you work with is more important candidly than the script" (Dec 2025). — [Motion expert page: Dara Denney](https://motionapp.com/library/expert/dara-denney/)
- Sarah Levinger: classic AIDA hooks now get scrolled past ("you can use AIDA and people just scroll right past it"); her answer is "micro-movies" and subtler selling. Ads work as "mirrors": "A brand is a mirror." Combine 2-3 identities per ad. Give the brand an enemy the customer can fight alongside it (Oct 2025 and Feb 2025 talks). — [Motion expert page: Sarah Levinger](https://motionapp.com/library/expert/sarah-levinger/)
- Savannah Sanchez: the first three seconds should be "as eye-catching as possible" to stop the scroll (Jul 2026). — [Motion talk](https://motionapp.com/library/talk/15-proven-ugc-ad-hooks-that-are-working-right-now-savannah-sanchez/)

### Inferences
- Use about 25% (feed) and 30% (Reels) as rough "fine" lines for cold Meta traffic, and judge a hook against the account's own average, as Motion advises. Never kill an ad on hook rate alone if it converts (Hott, Billo).
- The first frame itself is a hook element: Motion's <90% first-frame retention rule means the opening frame must not be a logo, black frame or slow fade.

### Gaps
- Motion's primary 2026 Creative Benchmarks PDF was not opened; medians here are secondhand.
- No primary Jake Abrahams, Foreplay, Insense or Meta Business Help page on the first 3 s was retrieved.
- No official TikTok 2024-2026 update of the 63% figure was found.

## 3. Retention: open loops, payoff timing, re-hooks, pacing, cut frequency, captions, ideal length

### Takeaway
After the hook, the body has to earn each next few seconds. Re-hook with a new reason to watch every 5-15 s, keep the visuals changing (one unnamed audit says every 1.5-2.5 s), close open loops late, and caption everything for sound-off. For cold traffic the sweet spot is 15-30 s (hold and ThruPlay drop sharply at 30-60 s), but longer ads also win when they are not boring. Sanchez's rule of thumb: ad length in seconds ≈ the audience's age (18-34 -> 18-34 s with quick cuts; older audiences can take longer ads).

### Cited Findings

**Hold / ThruPlay benchmarks (secondary roundup, 2025-2026)** — [AdSights](https://www.adsights.ai/resources/guides/video-creative-metrics-benchmarks-2026-guide)
- Hold (15s views / 3s views): feed cold 15-30 s ads 15-25% (~20%); Reels 18-30% (~23%); cold 30-60 s ads 8-18% (~12%); retargeting 25-40% (~30%); kill floor <10%.
- Motion bands (via Sepia Lab) for ThruPlays / 3s views: <30% needs improvement, 40-50% average, >60% strong.
- Watch depth (average play time / length, MHI Feb 2026): 15 s ads average 40-55%; 30 s ads 28-40%; 60 s ads 18-30%.
- ThruPlay rate: 15-30 s cold ~20-22%; 30-60 s cold ~12%; Triple Whale 2025 aggregate about 29%, sub-15 s 53.7%.
- Longer videos "lose roughly half of viewers before 15s on cold traffic" (Triple Whale 2025, via AdSights).

**Drop-off curve shapes (adlibrary 2026, via AdSights)** — [AdSights](https://www.adsights.ai/resources/guides/video-creative-metrics-benchmarks-2026-guide)
- Cliff at 3-4 s: the hook resolves but the body never starts.
- Slow bleed 5-15 s: pacing has no pattern interrupts.
- Cliff at 14-15 s: the offer or CTA framing breaks trust.
- Unnamed practitioner audit: high-retention creative changes visuals every 1.5-2.5 s; low-retention creative holds one shot 4-6 s. (Source unnamed, treat as directional.)

**Re-hooks and open loops**
- Re-hook every 5-15 s; if there is a welcome offer, put the CTA in the first 5-10 s and refer back to it later ("Remember that code..."). [OLDER, Apr 2023] — [UGC Social](https://ugcsocial.substack.com/p/hook-stacking-the-secret-to-longer)
- Denney: open loops keep viewers watching to "complete the loop". — [Motion: Dara Denney](https://motionapp.com/library/expert/dara-denney/)

**Length**
- Sanchez: "The age of your target demographic should be the length of your ad" (Jul 2025). "If you're targeting 18 to 34, your ad length should be 18 to 34 seconds and you have those super quick cuts" (Feb 2025). 40-60 year olds "have money and they have time" (Jul 2025). — [Motion: Savannah Sanchez](https://motionapp.com/library/expert/savannah-sanchez/). Her Jul 2026 examples ran about 14-27 s. — [Motion talk](https://motionapp.com/library/talk/15-proven-ugc-ad-hooks-that-are-working-right-now-savannah-sanchez/)
- Barry Hott: "Make the longest ad that you can that doesn't require any edits." — [Alex Cooper, Apr 2025](https://alexcooper.beehiiv.com/p/7-facebook-advertising-lessons-i-learned-from-barry-hott)
- Denney's "ad stacking": join 3-4 winning ads into one long video, or compile your best hooks into one ad; summary says 4-7 minute stacks are working; "there's no ad that's too long, just too boring" (Jan 2025 summary from a vendor page). — [Aimerce summary](https://www.aimerce.ai/topics/dara-denney-lazy-meta-ad-formulas-89). The "too long / too boring" line is also attributed to Hormozi (secondhand) — [OpusClip](https://opus.pro/blog/create-videos-in-alex-hormozi-style-the-ultimate-guide)
- Third-party Meta guidance: 15 s or less tends to capture attention best; 15-30 s also suggested. Meta allows up to 241 minutes, so this is a performance guideline, not a rule. Official Meta page not retrieved. — search summary of [JumpFly](https://www.jumpfly.com/blog/video-best-practices-for-meta-ads-maximizing-your-campaigns-impact/), [SaveMyLeads](https://savemyleads.com/blog/other/meta-video-ads-best-practices)

**Captions, text and sound**
- Design for sound-on and sound-off; always add captions or on-screen text so the message lands on mute (third-party summary of Meta guidance). — [AdStellar](https://www.adstellar.ai/blog/vertical-video-ad-best-practices)
- Captions/subtitles lift views 10-20%; sound effects and music lift engagement 20-30% (Billo, origin of the numbers not given). — [Billo](https://billo.app/blog/ugc-hooks/)
- TikTok: sound-on is the default; 40% of top-VTR auction ads use concise text overlays. [OLDER, 2020] — [TikTok for Business](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/en-US/blog/9-creative-tips-to-drive-auction-ad-performance)

### Inferences
- A 30 s UGC script outline that fits the data: 0-3 s hook (three layers) -> 3-8 s pay part of the hook and open a second loop -> re-hook around 8-12 s (proof/demo) -> re-hook around 15-20 s (objection or social proof) -> close the first loop and CTA around 22-30 s. Keep a new shot, angle or text beat every ~2 s.
- If retention cliffs at 3-4 s, rewrite the line right after the hook (the "bridge"), not the hook.

### Gaps
- No first-party second-by-second drop-off curve from Meta or TikTok was found; curve shapes above come from one secondary vendor.
- The "1.5-2.5 s per visual" audit is unnamed; no primary cut-frequency study was found.

## 4. Writing for the ear: pace, sentence length, tone, sounding unscripted

### Takeaway
Plan scripts at about 2.5-3 words per second (150-180 wpm): roughly 75-90 words for 30 s, about 40-45 for 15 s. Top practitioners prefer briefs and talking points over word-for-word scripts. Levinger: "If you want it to be natural, do not script it." Sanchez gives new creators a script but lets them ad-lib. TikTok's own guidance: short, direct, personal language, with the creator talking straight to camera. Clear beats clever (Hott). Customers' own words beat brand copy.

### Cited Findings
- Speaking pace: ~150 wpm (about 2.5 words/second) is the usual voiceover baseline; 150-180 wpm is common in TikTok/Reels and high-energy ads; fast hard-sell reads hit 180-200 wpm; relaxed reads 110-130 wpm. These figures come from voiceover-calculator vendors, not studies. — search summary of [Bread n Beyond](https://breadnbeyond.com/how-many-words-does-a-60-seconds-explainer-video-needs/), [Rows script calculator](https://rows.com/calculators/video-script-calculator), [IRPR word-count calculator](https://sounddesign.irpr.agency/tools/voiceover-word-count-calculator/), [Change Your Narrative](https://change-your-narrative.beehiiv.com/p/voice-over-script-timer-calculator)
- Levinger: "We are the director, we are not the screenwriter." "If you want it to be natural, do not script it." Brief creators on the character, not lines. [OLDER, Aug 2022 AMA] — [Motion: Sarah Levinger](https://motionapp.com/library/expert/sarah-levinger/)
- Sanchez: "I would definitely provide a script" for creators filming for the first time, but let them ad-lib in their own words; unscripted free rein only with creators you already work with (Jul 2026). — [Motion talk](https://motionapp.com/library/talk/15-proven-ugc-ad-hooks-that-are-working-right-now-savannah-sanchez/). "Let the creators do what they do best, which is creating raw content" (Jan 2026). — [Motion: Savannah Sanchez](https://motionapp.com/library/expert/savannah-sanchez/)
- TikTok: CTAs should use "short, direct, personal language"; creators breaking the fourth wall (talking to camera) appear in 1 in 3 top-VTR ads; nearly half of best-performing auction ads use an emotional, light, positive tone. [OLDER, 2020] — [TikTok for Business](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/en-US/blog/9-creative-tips-to-drive-auction-ad-performance)
- Hott: clear over clever; use customers' own language; native, low-polish "ugly" ads compete better in the feed. — [Alex Cooper, Apr 2025](https://alexcooper.beehiiv.com/p/7-facebook-advertising-lessons-i-learned-from-barry-hott); [Northbeam webinar recap](https://northbeam.io/post/unleashing-the-power-of-ugly-ads)
- Levinger mines Reddit and reviews (sorted into emotional categories) for the customer's words, because people are emotionally open about problems there (Sep 2024). — [Motion: Sarah Levinger](https://motionapp.com/library/expert/sarah-levinger/); [Foreplay webinar summary](https://www.foreplay.co/post/harnessing-psychology-in-creative-strategy-sarah-levingers-insights-on-consumer-behavior)
- AI-UGC vendor guidance: give the creator or avatar a strong opening line, one benefit and a CTA, and keep the wording loose enough to sound human. Over-polished, over-scripted delivery gets penalised whoever reads it. — search summary of [Segwise, UGC-style ads with AI](https://segwise.ai/blog/how-to-make-ugc-style-ads-with-ai)

### Inferences
- Ear rules a writer can apply (drawn from the sources above; specific numbers beyond wpm are not sourced): one idea per sentence; most sentences 4-12 words; contractions ("I'm", "it's", "didn't"); first person for the creator's story ("I"), switching to "you" for the call-out and CTA; read every line aloud and cut anything you would not say to a friend; leave room for ad-libs by writing bullets for the middle and exact words only for the hook and CTA.
- Word budget: 15 s ≈ 35-45 words, 30 s ≈ 75-90, 45 s ≈ 110-135 at 150-180 wpm (from the vendor wpm range).

### Gaps
- No primary 2024-2026 study was found on ideal reading level, sentence length, contractions, or first- vs second-person in UGC ads. The rules above are inference, not measured.
- No measured link was found between speaking pace and ad performance.

## 5. Common mistakes that make scripts sound like ads or like AI

### Takeaway
The ad-sounding mistakes practitioners name most: opening with the brand or a slow build-up instead of the viewer's problem; clever lines over clear ones; polished "ad voice"; jumping into a testimonial with no hook; old AIDA templates. The AI-sounding ones: generic formula scripts, plus the word and pattern tells Wikipedia's editors catalogue (e.g. "delve", "game-changing"-style hype, "not just X, but Y", stacked lists of three). Sanchez: "2025 is now how do we not sound like AI or look like AI."

### Cited Findings
- "Nobody cares what you have to say unless it relates to them clearly and instantly"; assume no one knows or cares about the brand; pretty ads get skipped; clever ads get ignored. — [Alex Cooper on Barry Hott, Apr 2025](https://alexcooper.beehiiv.com/p/7-facebook-advertising-lessons-i-learned-from-barry-hott)
- Copying competitor ads creates sameness; borrow from organic content instead. — [same](https://alexcooper.beehiiv.com/p/7-facebook-advertising-lessons-i-learned-from-barry-hott)
- Don't go straight into a testimonial without a hook first. — [Sanchez, Jul 2026](https://motionapp.com/library/talk/15-proven-ugc-ad-hooks-that-are-working-right-now-savannah-sanchez/)
- AIDA-style openings get scrolled past (Levinger, Oct 2025). — [Motion: Sarah Levinger](https://motionapp.com/library/expert/sarah-levinger/)
- Slow build-up before the hook: secondhand Hormozi example of cutting 3 s of build-up (40K -> 780K views, unverified). — [Skool](https://www.skool.com/lead-gen-mastery-4994/why-your-content-fails-in-the-first-3-seconds-and-how-to-fix-it)
- Sanchez on AI: "I feel like 2024 was the year of how can we use AI and 2025 is now how do we not sound like AI or look like AI." "One good ad is worth a thousand bad AI ads." "If the Motion data showed me that AI content worked better, I would be doing the AI content, but it's just not the case." (Oct 2025). Motion's summary says she argues AI scripts sound generic and follow the same formulas. — [Motion: Savannah Sanchez](https://motionapp.com/library/expert/savannah-sanchez/)
- Denney finds AI voiceovers "can really be indistinguishable" from normal ones (Apr 2025), but (per Motion's summary) advises against full AI UGC avatars. — [Motion: Dara Denney](https://motionapp.com/library/expert/dara-denney/)
- A synthetic creator has no lived experience of the product, which matters for performance and compliance. — search summary of [Segwise](https://segwise.ai/blog/how-to-make-ugc-style-ads-with-ai)

- Hook relevance: a high hook rate on the wrong audience is not success. — [Alex Cooper on Barry Hott](https://alexcooper.beehiiv.com/p/7-facebook-advertising-lessons-i-learned-from-barry-hott)

### Inferences
- A "slop filter" for UGC scripts: ban the Wikipedia words above plus hype words ("game-changer", "revolutionary", "elevate", "unlock", "seamless"); ban "not just X, it's Y"; ban stacked triplets of adjectives; ban rhetorical lead-ins like "Let's be honest" unless the creator really talks that way; require one concrete, checkable detail per claim (a number, a time, a body part, a place). This filter is my synthesis; only the Wikipedia list is sourced.
- In DTJ's context (rule 5 in CLAUDE.md), the "honest truth"/confessional and comment-reply hooks need real facts or real comments. They must never be invented.
