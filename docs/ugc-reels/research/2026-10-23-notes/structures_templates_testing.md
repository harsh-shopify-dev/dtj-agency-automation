# Short-form ad script structures, templates, briefing and testing (UGC / TikTok / Reels / Meta), 2024-2026

Researcher notes. Research date: 22-23 Oct 2026. Every finding below has an inline source. Templates in "Inferences" are my own paraphrased synthesis built from the cited beats; they are marked as such. Most sources are vendor or agency blogs (Billo, SideShift, Motion/Runneth, Foreplay) with a commercial interest, so treat their numbers as practitioner data, not audited studies. Note: Motion's blog URLs now redirect (301) to runneth.com, which says it is Motion's product.

## 1. Structures catalogue: beats, timings, who it suits, examples

### Takeaway
The near-universal skeleton is Hook (0-3 s) -> context/problem -> value (demo, story or list) -> proof -> one CTA, at 20-60 s and about 60-120 spoken words. Named structures are variations on how the middle is filled. The most detailed sourced catalogue (SideShift, Aug 2026) gives 12 frameworks with beats, funnel stage and length. Motion adds a separate layer of "mechanics" (how the ad makes meaning, e.g. Trojan Horse, Contrast Without Comment) that sit on top of any structure.

### Cited Findings

**Universal skeleton and length**
- Five-part timed skeleton for any framework: Hook 0-3 s (names problem, result or curiosity gap); Context 3-10 s (who it is for, why the creator cares); Value 10-25 s (delivers on the hook: demo, story or list); Proof 25-35 s (result, timeframe or on-camera demonstration); CTA in the final 5 s (one specific action, spoken and on screen). Talking points for a 30-45 s video: 60-110 words; over 150 words, cut context first, never hook or CTA — [SideShift, "UGC Script Template: 12 Frameworks That Convert in 2026", 6 Aug 2026](https://sideshift.app/blog/ugc-script-template)
- Billo calls Hook -> Problem -> Solution -> CTA the core UGC format; scripts are built for 30-60 s videos at about 75-120 words for a 30 s delivery; hook within the "first 3-5 seconds" — [Billo, Mastering UGC Scripts, 24 Sep 2026](https://billo.app/?p=45545)
- TikTok's official structure: "Have a hook, highlight your unique selling points, and end with a clear call-to-action"; prioritise the hook in the first 6 s and introduce the proposition in the first 3 s; on-screen text at 5-10 words per second; feature creators, employees or customers; DIY, not over-polished look; 9:16, at least 720p, with sound — [TikTok Ads Help Center, Creative best practices for performance ads](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/creative-best-practices)
- The often-quoted "21-34 s" ideal length is attributed to TikTok by SideShift, but TikTok's own help page does not state a length; unverified — [SideShift](https://sideshift.app/blog/ugc-script-template); contradicted/absent in [TikTok Help Center](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/creative-best-practices)
- Motion/Runneth's four components of a UGC ad: native-looking visuals (intentional, not overproduced); a hook in the first seconds then a short micro-story; specific, believable social proof; a conversational CTA. Facebook feed placements: 30-60 s problem-solution narrative — [Runneth (Motion), How to Create Effective UGC Ads, Melissa Rosen, undated, (c) 2026](https://runneth.com/blog/how-to-create-effective-ugc-ads)

**The 12 SideShift frameworks (beats in order; funnel; length)** — all from [SideShift, Aug 2026](https://sideshift.app/blog/ugc-script-template)
- Problem-solution: specific pain -> failed alternative -> product as fix -> specific result -> CTA. Cold/TOF. 20-35 s. Example: editing takes hours; app cuts it to ten minutes.
- Testimonial: timeframe + honest take -> unexpected benefit -> one minor drawback -> verdict + CTA. Trust, retargeting/MOF. 30-45 s. (Note the deliberate drawback as a credibility device.)
- Product demo: intro -> step 1 -> step 2 -> outcome -> time taken. Complex or new products/MOF. 30-60 s.
- Unboxing: arrival -> first detail noticed -> contents -> initial reaction. Launches, physical products/TOF. 20-40 s.
- Before/after: before state -> state after a timeframe -> "the only change was the product". Visible-result products/MOF. 15-30 s.
- Three reasons why (listicle): reason 1 -> reason 2 -> reason 3 (strongest last) -> CTA. Feature-rich products/MOF. 25-40 s.
- Comparison: test vs alternative over a timeframe -> criterion 1 winner -> criterion 2 winner -> overall pick and why. Decision stage/BOF. 30-60 s.
- Routine integration (day-in-the-life): routine intro -> steps -> product moment -> rest of routine. Lifestyle fit/TOF. 30-60 s.
- Myth-busting: "Stop doing X" -> conventional wisdom -> reality -> what worked instead. Crowded categories/TOF. 20-35 s.
- Storytime: inciting incident -> wrong assumption -> twist -> product discovery -> resolution. Emotional purchases/TOF-MOF. 30-60 s.
- Objection handling: acknowledge the questions -> price answer -> use-case answer -> "what's the catch" with an honest answer + CTA. Retargeting, higher price/BOF. 30-50 s.
- POV: relatable situation -> category + key benefit -> demo payoff. Organic reach, trends/TOF. 10-20 s.

**Founder formats (Alex Cooper, Ad Commerce / Creative Corner)** — [Alex Cooper, "I spent $3M on founder ads", 12 Apr 2026](https://alexcooper.beehiiv.com/p/i-spent-3m-on-founder-ads-here-are-the-5-founder-ads-that-work)
- Ugly Founder: phone to camera, unscripted, answering three questions: what problem I set out to solve, how the product solves it, what makes it different. Example Oyo Skincare shot in a bathroom; product not shown until about 1:20 ("storytelling first, product second").
- Founder Story: why I started -> the problem -> how the product solves it; sit-down with B-roll or fully organic. Example Henson Shaving.
- Podcast Founder: podcast set-up; expose an industry truth first, then the product as the fix; suits authority angles such as health. Example Dave Asprey / Danger Coffee.
- Warehouse Founder: founder tells the story inside the warehouse; "just feels authentic". Example Carpe's challenge ad (its top ad by impressions).
- Customer Call: founder calls a best customer on camera and lets them rave. Example Brez; a reported $1M+ spend is secondhand (via Nich Shackleford on X), unverified.

**Other named formats**
- Dara Denney's five effective formats (2024 podcast): feature-benefit callouts, "golden nugget" reviews, founder stories, statistics, educational ads; earlier talks list "Founder's Ad/Testimonials" and "Problem/Solution" — [OMG Commerce Ecommerce Evolution podcast ep. 287](https://www.omgcommerce.com/ecommerce-evolution-podcast/crafting-ad-creative-that-converts-combining-data-boldness); [Motion AMA with Dara Denney, Oct 2022 (older)](https://motionapp.com/library/talk/ama-creative-strategy-dara-denney/)
- Savannah Sanchez (The Social Savannah) segments named in a 2023 episode: the How-To/Tutorial, the Testimonial Mash-up, and the "Things I Wish I Knew Sooner" format (beats not published on the page; older, Feb 2023) — [Smart Marketer, Three Winning Formulas, 25 Feb 2023](https://smartmarketer.com/three-winning-formulas-for-your-ads-with-tiktok-expert-savannah-sanchez/). Her agency is described as shipping 200+ Meta and TikTok ads a week for about 50 brands with 40 in-house creators and 10 editors, iPhone-shot, and sceptical of AI scripts — [Motion expert profile](https://motionapp.com/library/expert/savannah-sanchez/)
- Us vs Them: side-by-side, before/after, or "with us / without us"; "them" can be a rival, the problem, an outdated way of thinking or the status quo; a variant pits a generic "Brand X" against the product in a timed test — [Bestever, Us vs Them ads 2025](https://www.bestever.ai/post/us-vs-them-ads); [Confect, 12 Us vs Them examples](https://confect.io/blog/Us-Vs.-Them-Ad-Examples)
- Street interview: associated with Street Poller Media (founder Shane Ginsberg); the argued advantage is built-in variety (different people, places, reactions), which he links to Andromeda's preference for diversity (single trade source; his own account) — [NetInfluencer on Street Poller Media](https://www.netinfluencer.com/how-street-poller-media-is-turning-street-interviews-into-performance-ad-infrastructure/); [HollywoodLife interview, 4 Sep 2026](https://hollywoodlife.com/2026/09/04/the-thousand-dollar-video-that-accidentally-started-an-industry-with-shane-ginsberg-founder-of-street-poller-media/)
- Billo vertical fits: beauty = transformations, before/after, emotional reactions; fitness = challenges or time-based testimonials; ecommerce = unboxings, usage demos, delivery experience — [Billo, Sep 2026](https://billo.app/?p=45545)

**Motion's creative "mechanics" (sit on top of any structure)** — [Motion Creative Mechanics library, updated 17 Apr 2026](https://motionapp.com/library/creative/mechanics/)
- The Implied Answer: the hook asks a slightly judging/curious question; the visuals answer it without words.
- The Social Witness: someone else notices the change (compliment, double-take, unprompted question).
- The Overheard Conversation: feels like something you were not meant to see (text thread, DM, group chat).
- The Reframe: validates a belief the viewer holds, then shifts it.
- The Borrowed Enemy: pins a problem, ingredient or feeling on a specific competitor/category.
- The Trojan Horse: looks like education, entertainment or a personal story until the final ~20%, when the product appears.
- The Contrast Without Comment: two realities side by side (before/after, old/new) with no explanation of which is better.
- "This and a...": the product shown next to something aspirational or emotionally resonant.

**Low-polish style**
- Barry Hott ("make ugly ads"): first reactions largely decide performance; native, low-production ads people will not skip; study the organic feed (angles, lighting, audio, pacing), not competitors; clarity over cleverness; assume nobody knows or cares about your brand; one full-funnel ad that hooks cold viewers then guides to action; "make the longest ad you can without needing edits" — [Alex Cooper, 7 lessons from Barry Hott, 10 Apr 2025](https://alexcooper.beehiiv.com/p/7-facebook-advertising-lessons-i-learned-from-barry-hott); [Practical Ecommerce interview](https://www.practicalecommerce.com/ugly-ads-perform-best-marketer-says)

### Inferences
(My paraphrased templates, built from the cited beats plus the SideShift skeleton. Timings assume a 30-40 s ad. Not directly sourced as a set.)
- **Problem-Agitate-Solve:** 0-3 s name the exact pain in the viewer's words; 3-10 s agitate (cost in money, time, embarrassment; the failed fix); 10-25 s product as the fix, shown in use; 25-33 s proof (result + timeframe, review overlay); last 5 s one CTA. Suits cold traffic and problem-aware buyers.
- **Before / After / Bridge:** 0-3 s the "after" result first (or split screen); 3-10 s the "before" life; 10-25 s the bridge: what changed and how it works; 25-35 s proof that it was only this change; CTA. Suits visible-result categories (skin, hair, cleaning, fitness).
- **Old way vs new way / Us vs Them:** 0-3 s "Still doing X?" or side-by-side; 3-20 s two or three head-to-head criteria, each with visual proof; 20-30 s the verdict; CTA. Keep "them" generic or the status quo to avoid claim risk.
- **Listicle ("3 reasons" / "3 mistakes" / "things I wish I knew"):** 0-3 s numbered promise; one point per 6-8 s, strongest last; the product as the answer to at least two points; CTA. "3 mistakes" is the myth-buster flipped into a list: each mistake = a common habit, why it fails, what to do instead (the product).
- **Testimonial / review:** 0-3 s verdict or surprising result; timeframe of use; unexpected benefit; one small honest drawback; verdict + CTA. The drawback raises believability.
- **Founder story:** 0-5 s the origin problem; why existing options failed; what I built and what makes it different; the founder's proof (process, warehouse, customers); soft-to-hard CTA.
- **POV / skit / character:** 0-2 s caption "POV: ..." relatable moment; 2-12 s the situation plays out; payoff shows the product solving it; short (10-20 s), entertainment first, product late (Trojan Horse mechanic).
- **Day-in-the-life / routine:** routine opens; the product moment sits in the middle; routine continues; mostly B-roll with voice-over; low hard-sell.
- **Unboxing / demo / ASMR:** arrival or close-up texture first; slow reveal of details; product in use with natural sound; one result; CTA. Lets the visuals carry proof; text overlays carry the claims for muted viewers.
- **Street interview / reaction / stitch:** a question or a stranger's reaction is the hook; real people try or guess the product; the reveal; reactions as social proof; CTA.
- **Comparison:** declare the test and timeframe; criterion-by-criterion winners on camera; overall pick + reason; CTA. Best for decision-stage and retargeting.

### Gaps
- No sourced beat-by-beat templates with timings were found specifically for: reaction/duet/stitch, skit/character, ASMR, "3 mistakes" and before/after/bridge as named; the templates above for these are my synthesis.
- Savannah Sanchez's three formulas are only known by segment title (2023); the beats were not published on the page found.
- Insense's own script templates were not found (searches returned other vendors).

## 2. Script documents, creative briefs, and angle vs concept vs format

### Takeaway
Pros brief with talking points and scenes, not word-for-word lines, and keep strategy (brief) separate from execution (script). Motion's stack gives the clearest vocabulary: Messaging Angle -> Creative Mechanic -> Hook -> Visual Format. "Concept" is the least settled term across sources. No primary source was found for a standard column layout (visual / spoken / on-screen text / B-roll), though briefs consistently pair each scene with footage and B-roll direction.

### Cited Findings
- Motion's stack: "Messaging Angle -> Creative Mechanic -> Hook -> Visual Format". A mechanic is "the cognitive or emotional move that makes the concept land"; it is neither the hook tactic (what the ad says) nor the visual format (how it looks); mechanic and format can be chosen in either order — [Motion Creative Mechanics, Apr 2026](https://motionapp.com/library/creative/mechanics/)
- Motion defines an angle as the "why should I care" logic behind an ad, specific, repeatable and summed up in a short phrase; distinct from a hook (how the ad opens); format is the container (static, video, carousel, UGC) — [Motion frameworks library](https://motionapp.com/library/frameworks/) (via search summary); [Motion 2026 Creative Strategy Bootcamp homework](https://motionapp.com/events/2026-creative-strategy-bootcamp/homebase/homework-week-3)
- Runneth (Motion) uses: ad format (UGC, static, video), hook type (problem-solution, social proof, curiosity), messaging angle (emotional trigger or pain point), audience approach; a "learned concept" is a test built on one component that has already moved results; an "ad family" explores one core insight through several formats, creators and visual styles. Old way: one UGC video with four headlines; new way: one angle across several formats — [Runneth, How to Do Creative Strategy in 2025](https://runneth.com/blog/how-to-do-creative-strategy-in-2025)
- Brief vs script: the brief is the strategy (goals, key messages, tone, deliverables, timeline); the script is the execution outline (flow, suggested visuals/tone, talking points without dictating every word) — [Billo, Sep 2026](https://billo.app/?p=45545)
- Six-block creator brief: 1 context; 2 deliverables and specs; 3 creative direction (angle, 2-3 hook options, must-say points, proof moments); 4 boundaries (claims, competitors, banned words, disclosure) kept in their own block; 5 rights and approval; 6 logistics (shipping, deadline, payment, contact). Write scenes and talking points, not word-for-word lines — [SideShift, Aug 2026](https://sideshift.app/blog/ugc-script-template)
- Influee's brief template splits the script into hook, talking points and CTA scenes, each paired with footage and B-roll direction — [Influee UGC brief template, 17 Jan 2025](https://influee.co/blog/ugc-brief-template)
- Briefs should give specific hook frameworks rather than "be authentic", list required key messages, platform specs and proven CTA language, and include do's and don'ts drawn from underperforming ads; quality checkpoints: concept approval, draft review, final format check, post-launch tracking; consider a base rate plus performance bonus for creators — [Runneth, How to Create Effective UGC Ads](https://runneth.com/blog/how-to-create-effective-ugc-ads)
- Dara Denney's modular UGC brief: ask for one testimonial plus about ten unedited B-roll clips rather than one finished video, so editors can mix variants; pull wording from customer reviews (2022, older) — [Motion AMA, Oct 2022](https://motionapp.com/library/talk/ama-creative-strategy-dara-denney/)
- Sarah Levinger: research starts from emotional identities pulled from customer reviews, not demographics; "Multiple Identity Matching" combines 2-3 identities per ad; a June 2026 talk introduces Customer / Brand / Presentation "Realities" and a T-E-E-P model (Trigger, Exploration, Evaluation, Purchase) to match formats to the buyer's state (from Motion listings; not verified against her own materials) — [Motion expert profile](https://motionapp.com/library/expert/sarah-levinger/); [Motion talk, Jun 2026](https://motionapp.com/library/talk/the-customer-psychology-research-system-that-powers-winning-ads-sarah-levinger/); [Foreplay summary](https://www.foreplay.co/post/harnessing-psychology-in-creative-strategy-sarah-levingers-insights-on-consumer-behavior)
- Foreplay's Briefs tool turns saved reference ads into briefs and storyboards with brand profile, reference ads and product links attached (vendor claim) — [Foreplay Briefs](https://www.foreplay.co/briefs); [Foreplay, AI creative briefs](https://www.foreplay.co/post/how-we-make-creative-briefs-with-ai-at-foreplay)

### Inferences
- A workable script document (my synthesis of the brief sources): one row per scene/beat with columns: Beat + time range | Visual (A-roll shot) | Spoken line or talking point | On-screen text (5-10 words/sec, per TikTok) | B-roll / insert | Proof element | Notes (claim boundary). Hooks live as 3-5 alternate rows for beat 1 so the body rows stay fixed.
- Working vocabulary for a team: Angle = the reason to care (one sentence, e.g. "costs less than takeout"); Concept = angle + mechanic + story (one distinct idea Meta would treat as different); Format = the container/production style (UGC talking head, founder, street interview, static); Hook = the first 1-3 s execution; Iteration = same concept, changed element.

### Gaps
- No primary agency document (Common Thread Collective, Pilothouse, Thesis) with a published script column format was found.
- No authoritative, non-vendor definition of "concept" was found.

## 3. Variant testing: modular hooks, hooks per body, iterating winners, Andromeda-era diversity

### Takeaway
Two different jobs: iteration (swap hooks, CTAs, creators on a proven body to find the best version) and diversification (genuinely different concepts for different personas or use cases). Meta said in Dec 2025 that it groups visually and thematically similar ads and treats them as one creative, so 20 hooks on the same shot read as one ad. Practitioners suggest 3-5 hooks per body as a starting test, with everything after second 3 identical; TikTok officially suggests 3-5 clearly different creatives per ad group.

### Cited Findings
**Meta (primary)**
- Meta: creative diversification is creating "unique campaign assets tailored to different potential customer personas or use cases"; iteration (manual A/B testing of small changes such as text CTAs on identical visuals) is a separate concept and "should not replace true creative diversification". Meta's system clusters ads that share key visual and thematic attributes and treats them as variations of one creative, sharing learnings at the creative level. Make ads "truly different in look, feel, storyline, and message"; diversify by format, persona/motivation (e.g. emotional appeal vs product features), creator (partnership ads) and asset type ("across text, image, and video"); build "a broad portfolio of assets". No fixed ad count given — [Meta, Demystifying Creative Diversification, 16 Dec 2025](https://www.facebook.com/business/news/demystifying-creative-diversification?locale=en_US)
- Andromeda is Meta's retrieval stage, "selecting ads from tens of millions of ad candidates into a few thousand relevant ad candidates" — [Meta Engineering, 2 Dec 2024](https://engineering.fb.com/2024/12/02/production-engineering/meta-andromeda-advantage-automation-next-gen-personalized-ads-retrieval-engine/)
- Meta's April 2025 post cites a brand that went from 3-4 new creatives a week to almost 50 with steady performance (a case example, not a target), and says Andromeda brought "an 8% improvement in ads quality" — [Meta, The Creative Advantage, 22 Apr 2025](https://www.facebook.com/business/news/the-creative-advantage-unlocking-the-power-of-diversification-with-meta-andromeda?locale=en_US) (as quoted by [NovoAds](https://novoads.ai/blog/creative-diversity-meta-ads))
- Ads Manager shows an estimated "Creative diversity" label (High/Medium/Low) comparing images and video thumbnails in an ad set; Meta marks it as in development — [Meta Business Help Center](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/2371176336684086?locale=en_US) (via NovoAds). A "roughly 60%" similarity threshold circulating from Billo is third-party, not a Meta figure — [NovoAds](https://novoads.ai/blog/creative-diversity-meta-ads)
- Jon Loomer: Meta's March 2025 messaging moved the main lever from niche targeting to creative diversification; targeting now largely happens inside the ad — [Jon Loomer, Meta Andromeda](https://www.jonloomer.com/meta-andromeda/); [Loomer podcast, 3 Sep 2025](https://pubcast.jonloomer.com/creative-diversification-is-the-new-targeting/)
- DataSlayer reports the old "no more than 6 ads per ad set" guidance disappeared in 2025 (not confirmed against Meta documentation) — [DataSlayer](https://www.dataslayer.ai/blog/meta-ads-changes-2025-83-updates-that-changed-facebook-advertising-forever)

**TikTok (primary)**
- "3-5 different creatives per ad group and 3-5 diversified ad groups per campaign"; "it is always better to use creatives with big differences, especially when testing"; refresh when delivery declines consistently, and add new creatives to the existing ad group — [TikTok Help Center](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/creative-best-practices)

**Practitioners**
- 3-5 hook variations per framework, with everything after the first three seconds kept identical — [SideShift, Aug 2026](https://sideshift.app/blog/ugc-script-template); test 3-5 hook variations per product, compare creator archetypes and formats, and track results per creator because some creators consistently outperform — [Runneth](https://runneth.com/blog/how-to-create-effective-ugc-ads)
- Billo method to turn one winning angle into 20 variations: identify the psychological promise; validate with 3 s hold and thumb-stop; break the message into claim, tension, proof, resolution; express the same tension through hook types (example mix: 5 short claims, 3 questions, 2 POV, 2 testimonial, 6 visual pattern interrupts, 2 CTA-led); 10-20 variations per cycle; refresh every 7-10 days or sooner when thumb-stop/early hold drops; Billo itself notes 20 hooks on the same shot and pace still read as one ad to Meta — [Billo, Ad Hooks That Scale, 24 Sep 2026](https://billo.app/blog/ad-hooks-variations/)
- Hook-count advice varies widely: one source pairs 10 visual x 10 verbal hooks over 2 bodies (200 combinations); others say 3-5, or 10 and warn 2-3 is too small. Run cells at the same time; vary only the opening (same length, ratio, audio, CTA); 4 purchases is noise; judge in stages: hook rate, then hold rate, then CPA. A hook that wins attention but loses sales over-promises vs the body — [Kalungi](https://www.kalungi.com/blog/test-ad-hooks-not-full-ads); [SocialPeta hook testing guide](https://socialpeta.com/en/blog/ad-hook-testing-guide); [AdLibrary, Meta Flex Ads for hook testing](https://adlibrary.com/posts/meta-flex-ads-hook-testing)
- Avoid "3 hooks that say the same thing in different words"; test different hook types (problem, curiosity question, bold claim) — [MakeInfluence](https://www.makeinfluence.com/en/academy/ugc-hooks-for-ecommerce-ads)
- Barry Hott: optimise for hook relevance, not hook rate alone; ads with hook rates below 15% have won — [Alex Cooper, Apr 2025](https://alexcooper.beehiiv.com/p/7-facebook-advertising-lessons-i-learned-from-barry-hott)
- Dara Denney treats a winning ad as the starting point for iterations (Laura Geller case: UGC-derived messaging improved an existing ad) and warns that messaging that wins in UGC does not automatically win in statics — [Motion talk, Feb 2025](https://motionapp.com/library/talk/this-one-ad-format-wins-for-dara-denney-s-clients/). To find winners: study long-running ads, look for the same strategy across products, adapt the message for new audiences (not just the visual), mine reviews for pains and transformations — [Foreplay, Unicorn ads with Dara Denney, c. Jan 2024](https://www.foreplay.co/post/how-to-identify-create-unicorn-ad-creative-with-dara-denney). Her metrics split (secondhand): primary KPIs (spend, results, CPR, ROAS) vs storytelling KPIs (hook rate, hold rate, watch time, frequency) — via LinkedIn posts summarised in search; unverified.
- Runneth: invest more in concepts with early positive signals; avoid reactive changes on small metric moves; audit past performance for winning formats and angles; analyse losers as closely as winners — [Runneth, Creative Strategy 2025](https://runneth.com/blog/how-to-do-creative-strategy-in-2025); [Runneth UGC](https://runneth.com/blog/how-to-create-effective-ugc-ads)
- Two-track planning: "Big Swings" (new concepts, ~30-day cycle) vs "Small Wins" (data-backed iterations); a handful of divergent concepts beats fifty near-identical variants — [Hawky](https://hawky.ai/blog/how-to-creative-strategy); [Hawky frameworks](https://hawky.ai/blog/creative-strategy-frameworks); pre-set rules: one variable per test, equal budget per variant, a spend/conversion threshold before calling a winner — [Hawky](https://hawky.ai/blog/creative-strategy-frameworks)
- Volume benchmarks from practice: The Social Savannah 200+ ads/week for ~50 brands — [Motion profile](https://motionapp.com/library/expert/savannah-sanchez/); Webtopia 120+ unique creatives/month for iHerb using Foreplay (vendor case study) — [Foreplay](https://www.foreplay.co/post/driving-scaled-creativity-and-performance-in-digital-advertising)

### Inferences
- Practical cadence that reconciles Meta and practitioners: each cycle, launch a few genuinely different concepts (different persona/angle + different mechanic + different creator/setting); on any concept that shows early signal, run 3-5 distinct hook types on the fixed body (iteration). Count "concepts" by what looks and sounds different, not by ad objects.
- Hook variants that only change the first spoken line over the same footage will likely be clustered by Meta as one creative; change the opening visual too (setting, framing, pattern interrupt) when the goal is reach into new pockets of audience.

### Gaps
- No primary Meta number for ads per ad set or concepts per week.
- No reliable public benchmark for hook rate or hold rate thresholds was found; sources either omit them or cite one agency's rules.
- Pilothouse, Common Thread Collective and Thesis do not appear to publish their testing frameworks openly; searches returned only CTC's generic page ([CTC creative strategy](https://commonthreadco.com/pages/creative-strategy)).
- Jake Abrahams: only a Foreplay author page for "Jake Abrams" (founder of Odyssey, May 2025 interview on turning one idea into many assets) was found; his method was not retrievable — [Foreplay author page](https://www.foreplay.co/authors/jake-abrams).

## 4. Proof devices, objection handling inside a 30-60 s ad, and CTAs

### Takeaway
Proof works when it is specific (named problem, timeframe, comparison to things tried) and aimed at one objection. In Billo's six months of UGC data, hard CTAs dominated top performers (74%), concrete offers beat vague urgency, and 6 of the top 10 ads also had a mid-video CTA. Motion argues for a conversational, recommendation-style CTA. The reconciled pattern: a clear, direct ask delivered in the creator's natural voice, once mid-video and once at the end.

### Cited Findings
- Billo, six months of creator video ads (count not disclosed), top performers tagged by CTA type: 74% of top performers used a hard CTA; all five highest-CTR entries did. Problem-solution CTAs (name the pain, pivot to the product) were the strongest type; direct commands second, and those naming the brand or URL beat generic "link below"; urgency/offer was most used with the widest spread, where concrete reasons (a specific discount or deadline) won and vague pressure ("they sell out fast") underperformed; personal-testimonial was the only soft CTA that consistently worked (as proof, not pitch). Six of the top 10 ads had a second CTA earlier in the video; the #1 ad used a mid-video line addressing hesitation, then the offer at the close. Example: "It's 50% off right now. Don't wait - go try it." (20.82% CTR) — [Billo Creative Insider, CTA examples, c. May 2026](https://billo.app/creative-insider/inspiration-ideas/cta-examples/)
- Motion/Runneth: CTA as friendly recommendation, not a sales demand (e.g. "If you're dealing with this same problem, definitely check this out"; a creator code); visual CTAs: creator typing a code, product held up with branding, pointing to the link, screen recording of finding it on site. ### Inferences
- Objection handling in 30-60 s: pick the one objection that kills the sale for this audience (price, "will it work for me", time, trust in an unknown brand) and answer it with a visual proof, not a claim: demo for "does it work", review screenshot or the creator's timeframe for "for someone like me", per-day cost or comparison for price, guarantee/COD/returns only if real (never invented). A mid-video line ("if you're thinking X...") doubles as the early CTA Billo saw in top ads.
- CTA placement: soft mention mid-video after the first proof, hard and specific at the end (brand/URL + a concrete, real offer if one exists). Retargeting/BOF scripts can use harder, offer-led CTAs; TOF scripts can lean on the testimonial-as-CTA.
- For DTJ: every number, review and offer in a script must come from the client's real data; Billo/Runneth examples show the pattern, not claims to reuse.

### Gaps
- No controlled, independent test comparing soft vs hard CTAs was found; Billo's data is its own platform's and undisclosed in size.
- No sourced guidance found on how many proof points fit in a 30 s ad, or on placing proof before vs after the demo.
