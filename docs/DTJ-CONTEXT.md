## Who DTJ's own pages are for
- DTJ's own landing pages target **Gujarati D2C founders**. Harsh's estimate (10 Oct 2026): of 100 founders, only about 20-25
  understand D2C technical terms (ROAS, CVR, funnels); the other 75-80 do not. The original page speaks to the technical minority.
  The planned variations are for the non-technical majority.
- They are a **visual audience**: they don't like reading, they "speak only numbers and results", and they are not interested in
  DTJ's process. Show **all** the proof: every case study, the work (ad reels) and every review.
- **How to write for them:**
  - plain founder English (not Gujlish), one pain point per page;
  - numbers and pictures first, very few words: no paragraphs, and no process beyond short step titles;
  - long sets of proof as swipe rows.
  - Pain points must come from Harsh or real lead data, never from assumptions.
- 12 Oct 2026: Harsh asked again for less text ("speak directly") on the variants. The original live page `/` keeps its full text.
- The skill `dtj-landing-variants` holds the details and the word budgets.

## What happens after someone fills the audit form
1. DTJ first checks the lead's quality.
2. Then the team contacts them (call, WhatsApp or email).

Tushar does **not** take a direct strategy call first (Harsh, 10 Oct 2026). The old page said "Direct strategy call with Tushar"
in two places; it was removed from `/` and `/v2` of the DTJ landing page.

In any landing page, ad or variant copy, describe the next step as "we check your details first, then our team gets in touch". Do
not write a response time, or who calls, until DTJ confirms it.

## Landing pages: no distractions
The page has one job: getting a founder to the audit form. Harsh had the `/v2` footer (phone, website, social links, copyright)
removed on 10 Oct 2026: "we don't want our clients to get distracted".
- **Leave out:** footer, nav menu, social or outbound links, links that open new tabs or go to other pages (including the logo
  linking home).
- **What a page legally needs** (for example a privacy policy link) goes in the smallest possible place, such as the form's
  consent line.
- **Before adding a section,** ask whether it moves the visitor toward the form. If not, leave it out.
